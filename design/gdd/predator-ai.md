## Overview

Predator AI is the server-authoritative behavioral core that turns ecological disturbance into consequence: a single apex hunter, one instance per server, that reads the disturbance field the squad creates and commits to whoever is loudest. It is specified as three data-driven submodules — **Perception** (what the predator can sense: the hottest hotspot via `DisturbanceService:GetHottestHotspot`, line-of-sight raycasts, and the squad-aggregate quiet level via `GetSquadAggregateT`), **Decision** (a deterministic 4-state machine — Patrol → Investigate → Hunt → Disengage — with telegraphed transitions, dwell-time floors, and a no-randomness target-selection rule), and **Navigation** (throttled pathfinding toward the chosen hotspot within a server tick budget). The data-driven split exists so post-launch predator variants are cheap re-parameterizations, not rewrites.

To the player, this system *is* the horror. The predator is the planet answering back — heard before it is seen (its audio reach deliberately exceeds its visual detection range), summoned not by scripting but by the squad's own gathering, lights, sprints, and beacon. Its behavior is **readable, never random**: every state change is globally telegraphed (warning cue → state change → a minimum state duration), it never one-shots, and quiet play visibly cools it down — so the squad always understands *why* it was found and always has a lever to lose it. Without Predator AI there is no teeth to "The World Watches" and no cost to being loud: the disturbance system would be an unread meter, oxygen pressure would be the only threat, and the game's entire differentiator — *the hunt rides your noise* — would not exist.

> *Behavior-level specification only. The locomotion driver (Roblox CharacterController vs legacy Humanoid) and the concrete PathfindingService usage are implementation decisions deferred to an architecture ADR.*

## Player Fantasy

**You are not the hunter. You are the wound.** The planet was here first, and it was quiet first — and everything you do to survive, every canister you crack and lantern you raise and panicked sprint you make, is a fever it runs to flush you out. The first time you notice it is not a jump scare: you're heads-down over a node, the work going well, and underneath the ambience you catch it — breathing, low and organic, *closer than it was.* That is the whole game in one beat. The horror is not random. The planet is answering the noise you made.

**It cannot be fought, and was never meant to be — it is read.** This is not a creature you beat; it is one you *lose.* It telegraphs everything: every escalation announces itself, every state-change is earned, nothing is a cheap shot, and it never ends you in a single blow. You learn its language — the swell of subsonic pressure when it commits to the loudest of you, the stretch of silence it takes to give up — until you stop reacting and start anticipating. Survival is literacy, not reflex, and going quiet is always a lever in your hands.

**And the dread is never yours alone — it is the squad's.** It hunts the loudest point, so four people learn each other in the silence: a held position, a pointed direction, the discipline of someone who freezes instead of bolting. The predator doesn't only threaten the crew; it *creates* it. The beacon-window standoff — when the signal is lit and it bears down at full weight while you all hold the line together — is the moment four players become a crew.

> *Reading the predator belongs to everyone: the heard-before-seen contract carries full visual redundancy — directional indicators and breathing/footstep captions — so a deaf or hard-of-hearing player reads the hunt exactly as well. Accessibility here is fantasy-positive, designed in, not bolted on.*

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **Instance & lifecycle.** Exactly **one** predator instance per server, server-authoritative, spawned at session start in **Patrol** (the world is already watching — it does not spawn on a threshold). It never despawns mid-run; on full-squad wipe or run-end it is cleaned up with the run.
2. **Perception is two-channel.** (a) **Hearing = the disturbance field** — the predator's only sense of player-generated noise. At the 4 Hz decision tick it calls `DisturbanceService:GetHottestHotspot(predatorPosition, PREDATOR_SEARCH_RADIUS, minimumTier)`, the squad-aggregate accessor, and reacts to `TierCrossedEvent`. **The predator senses player disturbance *only* through this field API** (hard rule — guarantees the Dampener Coil and all emission modifiers always work upstream; the predator must never add a direct player-raycast targeting path that would bypass an emission reducer). **Aggregate semantics (R5, SPLIT — `GetSquadAggregateT` returns the `max` over the non-excluded alive set; NOT a mean or sum):** ESCALATION reads (Patrol→Investigate via `TierCrossedEvent`, and Investigate→Hunt eligibility) use `max(player T)` — any single loud player summons the hunt (Pillar 1, non-negotiable). The **Hunt→Disengage quiet-check** reads the squad-aggregate **EXCLUDING the locked target**: `squadQuietT = GetSquadAggregateT(excludeLockedPlayer)` (= `max` over alive players minus the locked target) — the chased player's forced-survival sprint emission must NOT block the squad from earning a disengage by going quiet. **SOLO / last-alive fallback (R1):** when the exclude-set is empty (squad size 1, OR the last non-locked player dies mid-Hunt leaving only the locked player alive), the quiet-check reads the lone player's own T (no exclusion — solo must break LOS + stop + go quiet to disengage; hardest case, by design; the mid-Hunt transition into this fallback when a squadmate dies is an explicit edge case). **Squad-size-scaled quiet threshold (RR-2 — Pillar-1 fix at small squads):** the quiet-check compares `squadQuietT` against `effectiveQuietThreshold(n)`, where `n` = the count of players contributing to the check (alive minus excluded). Because at squad size 2 the check collapses to a *single* active survivor's T (a moving-but-not-loud survivor would otherwise pin the timer ≥0.30 forever and break "quiet is a reliable lever"), the threshold scales **up** at small squads: `effectiveQuietThreshold = PREDATOR_QUIET_THRESHOLD_BY_ALIVE[n]`, default `{1: 0.45, 2: 0.38, ≥3: RETREAT_DROPOUT_THRESHOLD = 0.30}` (exact small-squad values prototype/balance-tuned — see Tuning Knobs + OQ.12). At `n ≥ 3` the baseline 0.30 is unchanged. **RD-1 structural guarantee (Pillar-1, n=2 — round-4):** the small-squad values are NOT free parameters — they are constrained: `effectiveQuietThreshold(2)` MUST be tuned strictly **above** the `T` a non-locked player generates while *moving between nodes at the intended gather cadence* (doing necessary work without actively gathering-loud), so that "quiet is a reliable lever" is *structurally* true at the 2-player primary squad, not merely aspirational. Validating this bound against real per-player emission is a **BLOCKING prototype gate (OQ.12)** — if no threshold can separate "working quietly" from "loud," the disengage model must change. The loud kiter who holds the lock pays for the squad's earned Disengage with their **own exposure** (they cannot simultaneously craft), so the n=2 "kite-and-craft" rotation costs one of the two players entirely — a deliberate division-of-labor cost, not a free exploit; its reliability is bounded by OQ.13. **RD-6 timer-vs-n-change (round-4):** the 20 s sustain timer is reset ONLY by a tick where `squadQuietT ≥ effectiveQuietThreshold(n)`; a change in `n` itself (a squadmate dying OR respawning mid-Hunt) **never** resets the timer — the next tick is simply evaluated against the new threshold. PA **cannot** compute per-player exclusion itself under the Core-Rule-2 field-only hard rule, so ED must expose `GetSquadAggregateT(excludePlayer)` (or per-player T) — see Dependencies + Open Questions. (b) **Sight = raycasts** — ≤16 rays at 4 Hz, allocated **`min(aliveCount, 4)` direct-LOS rays** (one per alive player, **capped at 4** — above 4 alive players the direct rays go to the 4 closest-to-predator; this keeps the budget fixed at ≤16 regardless of squad size) + 4 forward-arc + 8 shoulder-offset. **Audio range exceeds visual range** (binding constraint): the predator navigates toward a hotspot it cannot see, but **a hit requires unobstructed LOS** to the target.
3. **Targeting (deterministic, no RNG).** The predator pursues the **hottest hotspot** within `PREDATOR_SEARCH_RADIUS` (200 studs). Total-order tie-break: higher field value wins (equal within `HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` = 0.005) → else closer to the predator (squared distance) → else lowest stable `hotspot.id`. If `GetHottestHotspot` returns nil → Patrol fallback (ED E.5/E.5b).
4. **Player lock.** On entering Hunt, the predator locks the **closest alive player TO THE HOTSPOT** (RR-1 — canonical): `lockTarget = argmin` of `dist²(hotspot, player.HumanoidRootPart)` over alive players in search radius; tie broken by ascending `UserId`. **(RR-1 reconciliation: the lock distance is measured to the HOTSPOT, not to the predator — D.3 and H.10 are reconciled to this; "the hunt rides your noise" means the predator commits to whoever made the loudest point, which is the player nearest the hotspot.)** The lock is **NOT permanently sticky** — it is re-evaluated periodically and on range. **Evaluation cadence / precedence (single decision point, RR-1/round-3):** all lock-change decisions — both the periodic re-eval (a) and the range-release (b) — are evaluated **ONLY at the `PREDATOR_LOCK_REEVAL_INTERVAL` boundary** (the ~3 s periodic tick is the *one* lock-decision point; neither is checked per-Heartbeat nor per-4 Hz-perception-tick). **Immediate (non-cadence) exemptions (FIX 15 — closed list):** the following clear/drop the lock the *moment* they occur, NOT at the next boundary: (1) target death, (2) target disconnect, (3) `RunEnded`, (4) a nil / decayed / stale-aged hotspot at the re-evaluation-before-lock guard on Hunt entry, and (5) FSM exit out of Hunt (→ Disengage or → nil-timeout Patrol). The cadence governs ONLY periodic *switching between two live targets* (paths a and b below); these five events are immediate. Within a lock-decision, a switch fires only if the **SAME challenger hotspot** (identified by stable `hotspot.id`) satisfies the R4 margin for `≥ PREDATOR_TARGET_DEBOUNCE_TICKS` **strictly consecutive** *perception* ticks observed since the prior lock-decision (debounce gate). **Counter scope (FIX 15):** the debounce counter is keyed to the challenger `hotspot.id`; any tick where the leading challenger changes identity, OR where the challenger fails the R4 margin, **resets the counter to zero** — so two *different* hotspots each winning one tick never sum to a switch, and a single below-margin tick within the window restarts the count. (a) **Periodic re-evaluation (R3):** at each interval the lock re-evaluates and switches to the genuinely-hotter hotspot/player per the R4 margin below — a quiet kiter who has outrun the predator loses the lock and the predator re-targets the now-loudest players. **Lock-transfer telegraph (RD-3 — round-4):** a lock transfer is **globally telegraphed** like a state change — it fires a distinct lock-transfer cue (audio + full visual redundancy, Visual/Audio + UI Req) and updates BOTH the released player's and the newly-locked player's HUD (with the YOU/SQUAD min-display floor), so a mid-Hunt re-target is never a silent "cheap shot." A lock transfer is not an FSM *state* change, but it IS a telegraphed escalation — this preserves the "every escalation announces itself" contract of the Player Fantasy. (b) **Range release (R4):** the lock releases when the target exceeds `PREDATOR_LOCK_RELEASE_RANGE` (~60 studs) **AND** another hotspot's field exceeds the locked hotspot's *current* field by more than `PREDATOR_RETARGET_FIELD_MARGIN` (default **0.07**; must be **> the max single-tick oscillation Δ ≈ 0.06** AND > `HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` 0.005, so a single ED-hysteresis oscillation cycle cannot clear the margin); it does **not** release on range alone. (c) The lock also clears when the FSM exits Hunt, or when the target dies/disconnects — in which case the **lock-clear is owned by `DisturbanceService:ReleasePredatorLock`**, **not** by PC firing it directly (FIX 8). **Two trigger paths into `ReleasePredatorLock` (FIX 17):** (i) a **player-death/disconnect** is routed through DisturbanceService's `OnPlayerDied` fan-out; (ii) a **non-death Hunt-exit** (quiet-earned Disengage or nil-timeout→Patrol) is triggered by DisturbanceService's subscription to PA's Channel A `OnPredatorStateChanged` BindableEvent — on any `fromState == "Hunt"` transition DisturbanceService calls `ReleasePredatorLock(predatorId)`. Both paths funnel through the single `ReleasePredatorLock` owner, so the `{locked = false}` fire still originates from exactly one place (preserves H.40); PredatorService never fires the clear itself. Before firing the `{locked = false}` clear, `ReleasePredatorLock` **guards `if player and player.Parent`** (the locked player may already have left the game on a disconnect-race) so the RemoteSignal clear is never fired against a nil/removed `Player` (NET-3-B1). A `ReleasePredatorLock` call for a player who is **not** the current lock target is a **no-op** (no re-target, no spurious `PredatorEncountered`). `PredatorEncountered` analytics fires **once per player per session** (session-persistent `_seenPlayers` dedup — H.13; a re-lock after release/respawn does NOT re-fire it — supersedes the earlier loose "once per lock" wording), carrying `lockedPlayerId` and `distanceToHotspot`.
5. **Movement.** Navmesh-bound only (no teleport, no shortcut that bypasses the navmesh). Per-state speed: Patrol 6, Investigate 10, **Hunt 16**, Disengage 6 studs/s. A config-validation gate asserts `PREDATOR_PATROL_SPEED < PREDATOR_INVESTIGATE_SPEED < WALK_SPEED (12) < PREDATOR_HUNT_APPROACH_SPEED (16) < SPRINT_SPEED (20)` — sprinting is a reliable escape, walking is not (Pillar 1); Investigate must stay strictly sub-walk; and Patrol must stay strictly below Investigate so the predator visibly *picks up speed* when it finds something (the `PREDATOR_PATROL_SPEED < PREDATOR_INVESTIGATE_SPEED` clause is new in round-3 — at the un-gated extremes Patrol 10 ≥ Investigate 8 would invert the read). Investigate (10) is deliberately sub-walk so a player who caused a disturbance still has time to leave before the predator arrives.
6. **Attack & kill (never one-shots).** On contact within `PREDATOR_MELEE_RANGE` (6 studs, strictly less than `BEACON_HOLD_RADIUS` 12) **with LOS**, a Lunge deals `≈ 30% of PLAYER_MAX_HP`; `ATTACK_COOLDOWN` = 3 s between attempts; **`hitsToDeath = ceil(1 / DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT)` hits to HP=1** (4 hits at the 0.30 default, ~9 s if every hit lands uninterrupted; 5 hits at 0.20, 3 at 0.40). Hard one-shot guard: `effectiveDamage = min(damage, Humanoid.Health − 1)`, so a single hit can never reduce a full-health player to 0. A hit applies a **bounded outward impulse capped at `PREDATOR_BC4_MAX_KNOCKBACK` (8 studs)** (the `_BC4` suffix is a misnomer inherited from the PC GDD — the cap applies in all states; flagged to the PC author). After any hit the player has the cooldown window to sprint out of melee range and break the attack chain — "you lose it, you don't tank it." A startup config-gate asserts **`ATTACK_COOLDOWN ≥ 2.0 s`** (its safe-range floor): below 2 s the post-hit reaction window is unrecoverable on an iPhone-SE-class device (at Hunt 16 the player has ≈`PREDATOR_MELEE_RANGE/PREDATOR_HUNT_APPROACH_SPEED` = 0.375 s to clear melee plus the cooldown; a sub-2-s cooldown leaves no net mobile margin).
7. **Beacon-window commit.** During BC4 (from `beaconActivated == true` until `runOutcomeResolved == true`), the predator reads the beacon hotspot at `max(actualBeaconT, PREDATOR_BC4_COMMIT_T_FLOOR = 0.65)` — it commits to the beacon at peak threat. **During BC4 the beacon-commit OVERRIDES the quiet path: there is NO quiet-disengage in the finale** (R2 — this inverts the prior behavior). The squad holds the line and survives the window, or loses; the only outcomes are hold-and-survive or full-wipe. **BC4 defeat source (RR-3):** the **no-one-shot guard remains in force during BC4** (the predator never reduces a player below HP=1 by attack), so the predator alone **cannot** wipe the squad — a player pinned at 1 HP is not killed by further lunges (`effectiveDamage = min(damage, Health − 1) = 0` at HP=1). The **defeat condition (full-wipe) is driven by oxygen depletion and/or the beacon-window timer — both Crafting/RM-owned — NOT by predator damage.** The predator's role in the finale is to force exposure and movement that accelerates oxygen loss and disrupts the hold; the *killing* is the planet running out the squad's air, not the jaws. **RD-4 mechanism (round-4 — closes "RR-3 is an assertion without a mechanism"):** RR-3 is only coherent if the finale is actually loseable, so PA commits a **named cross-GDD constraint** (Crafting/RM-owned — named here so it cannot be silently violated): **`BEACON_WINDOW_DURATION` and the BC4 oxygen-depletion rate MUST be tuned so that an undamaged squad pinned at HP=1, taking zero further predator damage, runs out of oxygen *before* the beacon window ends** — i.e. a 1-HP standoff is loseable on air, never unloseable on a long window. PA owns only the *pressure* (forcing exposure/movement that accelerates oxygen loss); validating this relationship is a **BLOCKING Crafting-BC4 / RM obligation (OQ.13)**, and a cross-GDD startup config-gate asserts it once both systems pin their values. (Cross-ref Crafting & Items BC4 / Resource Management oxygen — see Dependencies. This resolves the "1-HP immortality ⇒ unloseable BC4" gap: the predator was never the wipe agent.) The `max(actualBeaconT, 0.65)` clamp applies **ONLY to the beacon-hotspot field read** used for commit/navigation — it does **not** apply to the squad-quiet-check (and under R2 the quiet→Disengage path is suppressed in BC4 anyway, so the clamp is purely a field-read floor). The FSM does not change during BC4; only the beacon hotspot's effective field floor does. (Satisfies ED forward-obligation F8: the predator continues its hotspot path on a capped beacon rather than entering a special state.)
8. **Telegraphs & state/lock channels (two distinct channels — ED.F.2a row 3).** Every state change is telegraphed in the form `warning cue → state change → minimum state duration`; the audio cue precedes the visual reveal; a directional indicator + breathing/footstep captions provide full visual redundancy (accessibility is designed in). Predator state is published over **two distinct, independent channels** (replacing the prior single "globally-broadcast predator state" model; reverse-cites **ED.F.2a row 3**):
   - **Channel (A) `OnPredatorStateChanged`** — a **server-internal BindableEvent**. Payload `{predatorId: string, fromState: string, toState: string, timestamp: number}`. Fired by Predator AI on **every** state-machine transition. **Server-internal ONLY** — never replicated to clients, never declared on any Knit Client surface. `DisturbanceService` subscribes to it for its C.1.11/E.23 defense-in-depth purge.
   - **Channel (B) `OnPredatorLockChanged`** — a **client-facing Knit `RemoteSignal`**, declared by Predator AI on its own `Service.Client` table. Fired `OnPredatorLockChanged:Fire(targetedPlayer, payload)` (Knit `RemoteSignal` API — **not** `:FireClient`). It targets the **locked player's** HUD for the predator-locked-on indicator. **Ownership (round-9 DR-3):** Predator AI fires the `{locked = true, ...}` case on its own state-into-Hunt-with-target transitions; the `{locked = false}` (clear) fire is performed by **`DisturbanceService:ReleasePredatorLock(predatorId)` itself**, NOT by Predator AI.
   - **Independence:** the BindableEvent fires on state transition; the RemoteSignal fires when the lock resolves to a player. There is **no single source of truth** between them — they are deliberately decoupled (a state change is not always a lock change, and vice-versa).
   - **Reference acquisition (handshake module).** `DisturbanceService` acquires the `OnPredatorLockChanged` handle via **PUSH registration** through a handshake module at `src/server/services/DisturbanceService/RegistrationHandshake.luau` (NOT via `Knit.GetService` pull). The module has exactly **5 canonical exports**: `RegisterPredatorLockChangedSignal`, `RegisterPredatorStateChangedSignal`, `setOnPredatorStateChangedHandler`, `getOnPredatorLockChangedSignal`, `getOnPredatorStateChangedBindable`. During Predator AI's `KnitStart` it calls `handshake.RegisterPredatorLockChangedSignal(self.Client.OnPredatorLockChanged)` and `handshake.RegisterPredatorStateChangedSignal(self._onPredatorStateChanged)` (order-independent). **Register-time-subscribe + pending-INVOCATION queue (round-3 failure-mode hardening):** the `:Connect` to `OnPredatorStateChanged` happens inside `RegisterPredatorStateChangedSignal`'s body (this closes the `KnitStart` inter-service ordering race). The connected handler does NOT invoke the application handler directly; it appends each fire to a **bounded pending-invocation queue** (cap `HANDSHAKE_PENDING_QUEUE_MAX`, default 64; on overflow it drops the oldest and logs a warning — never errors). `DisturbanceService`'s `KnitStart` imports the same module and runs sub-steps in **STRICT order**: (4-a) read `getOnPredatorLockChangedSignal` → store; (4-b) read `getOnPredatorStateChangedBindable` → store; (4-c) **LAST**: `setOnPredatorStateChangedHandler(self._handlePredatorStateChanged)`. The handler-setter MUST be last; it installs the application handler and then **drains the pending-invocation queue** (replaying buffered fires in order) — the buffered closures read the slots written in 4-a/4-b. **Lazy-read contract (FIX 16 — required for correctness):** the enqueued closures capture *references* to the slot variables, NOT their values at enqueue time; they read the slot values only at drain/invocation time (during 4-c), by which point 4-a and 4-b have already written non-nil. Eager value-capture at enqueue (when the slots may still be nil) is a defect — a fire buffered before 4-a/4-b run would capture nil and nil-deref on drain. **Never-installed safety:** if `setOnPredatorStateChangedHandler` is *never* called (e.g. DisturbanceService's `KnitStart` errors before 4-c), `OnPredatorStateChanged` fires accumulate in the bounded queue and are dropped past the cap — there is **no nil-deref**: the connected handler always guards `if self._handlePredatorStateChanged then …(invoke)… else …(enqueue)…`. **Late-registration safety:** if `RegisterPredatorStateChangedSignal` arrives *after* `setOnPredatorStateChangedHandler` (interleaved `KnitStart` order), the registration still connects the SAME handler closure (the one that guards `if self._handlePredatorStateChanged then invoke else enqueue`) — there are NOT two control-flow shapes. Because the handler is already installed by the time any fire arrives, the guard takes the invoke branch and nothing is queued. **Single canonical guard (FIX 16):** registration-time code only *connects* the guarded handler; the invoke-vs-enqueue decision lives entirely inside that one connected handler, evaluated at fire time — so the never-installed and late-registration paths are the *same* code, differing only in which guard branch runs.
9. **Death attribution.** On lock, the predator stores the **top-3 contributing `emissionId`s** from the `HotspotResult` (ED.C.1.11) so the locked player's death screen can name the dominant disturbance type, its magnitude, and seconds-before-death (per the game concept's death-state teaching layer).

### States and Transitions

The Decision submodule is a deterministic 4-state machine. Names are disambiguated from the ED disturbance tiers ("Disengage" was renamed from "Retreat" to avoid collision with the ED tier "Retreat" at t=1.00).

| State | Behavior | Speed (studs/s) | Transitions out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patrol** | Waypoint patrol; reads the field each decision tick | 6 | → **Investigate** when a hotspot crosses **Tense (0.30)** (`TierCrossedEvent`). **Stale-signal/nil-recovery (FIX 6):** if `TierCrossedEvent(Tense)` fires but the subsequent `GetHottestHotspot` returns nil (decayed/out-of-radius), the predator **STAYS in Patrol** (stale signal — no transition). |
| **Investigate** | Path to the hotspot; sight-confirm at ≤40 studs; **minimum 8 s dwell** before Hunt is eligible | 10 | → **Hunt** when the **hottest hotspot's `fieldValue` ≥ Hunt (0.65)** (per-hotspot, ED-canonical) *and* ≥8 s dwell elapsed. **Re-evaluation-before-lock guard (FIX 6):** on Hunt entry, re-query `GetHottestHotspot`; if nil/decayed, do NOT lock against a stale hotspot — start the nil→Patrol timer instead. → **Patrol** if `GetHottestHotspot` is nil for `INVESTIGATE_FALLBACK_DURATION` (**15 s** default) |
| **Hunt** | Lock the closest player **to the hotspot** (RR-1); pursue; Lunge on contact + LOS | 16 | → **Disengage** when `squadQuietT = GetSquadAggregateT(excludeLockedPlayer) < effectiveQuietThreshold(n)` (squad-scaled, RR-2; `n` = alive-minus-excluded count; baseline `RETREAT_DROPOUT_THRESHOLD` 0.30 at n≥3, higher at n≤2) sustained **≥20 s** (reset semantics — any tick `≥ effectiveQuietThreshold(n)` restarts the timer). **BC4 exception: suppressed** — during BC4 the quiet→Disengage path does NOT fire (R2; beacon-commit overrides). → **Patrol** if the hottest hotspot is nil for **15 s** |
| **Disengage** | **Committed 12 s cooldown** (`PREDATOR_DISENGAGE_DURATION`): move to the farthest-from-squad navmesh waypoint and ignore all hotspots — a guaranteed breathing-room window the squad can rely on (OUTSIDE BC4 only) | 6 | → **Patrol** on timer expiry (resumes patrol waypoints; may immediately re-Investigate on the first tick that reads a hotspot ≥0.30 — Disengage grants a window, not immunity) |

- **Hysteresis** ±0.03 on every tier boundary (ED-canonical). All tier thresholds are ED-owned tuning knobs; this GDD consumes them, it does not redefine them.
- **Two distinct "we lost it" feels, both preserving the 4-state count:** Hunt→**Disengage** (lost via sustained quiet — committed cooldown, distinct retreat audio) vs. Hunt/Investigate→**Patrol-timeout** (the hotspot decayed to nil — different, quieter audio so the squad can tell the two apart). No fifth "Searching" state is needed.
- **Telegraph + minimum state duration** gate every transition so no state can flicker at the boundary; the minimum-duration floor is what makes the global telegraph readable.
- **Clear previously-navigated-to position (FIX 6, ED.F.2a row 1 + F6).** The predator MUST clear its prior nav target on each of: Investigate→Patrol fallback (`INVESTIGATE_FALLBACK_DURATION` expiry), predator respawn, and **any** state-exit out of Hunt/Investigate/Disengage — so a stale navigation target never leaks across states.
- **Spawn:** the predator exists from session start in Patrol (not spawned on a threshold).

### Interactions with Other Systems

## Formulas

All values are tuning knobs (full safe ranges and extreme behaviors in Section G). Specialist source: systems-designer (numeric model) + game-designer (kill/commit rulings).

### D.1 — Per-state movement speed & the escape-guarantee inequality

`PREDATOR_SPEED(state) ∈ { Patrol: 6, Investigate: 10, Hunt: 16, Disengage: 6 }` studs/s.

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WALK_SPEED` | float | 12 (PC-owned) | Player walk speed |
| `SPRINT_SPEED` | float | 20 (PC-owned) | Player sprint speed |
| `PREDATOR_HUNT_APPROACH_SPEED` | float | 13–19 (def 16) | Hunt closing speed |
| `PREDATOR_INVESTIGATE_SPEED` | float | 8–11 (def 10) | Investigate speed — strictly sub-walk |

**Invariant (config-validation gate, asserted at startup):** `WALK_SPEED < PREDATOR_HUNT_APPROACH_SPEED < SPRINT_SPEED` — strict on both sides — **AND** `PREDATOR_INVESTIGATE_SPEED < WALK_SPEED` (FIX 4 — Investigate must stay strictly sub-walk).
**Output / behavior:** at 16, a sprinter gains `20 − 16 = 4` studs/s (8 s of stamina → ~32-stud lead → breaks line of sight); a walker loses `16 − 12 = 4` studs/s.
**Extremes:** at `= SPRINT_SPEED` it is an inescapable treadmill (Pillar-1 break); at `≤ WALK_SPEED` walking becomes safe and the core mechanic inverts. Investigate (10) is intentionally sub-walk.

### D.2 — Hotspot selection (deterministic total order)

`preferred(a, b)`: if `|field(a) − field(b)| ≥ HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` → the higher `field` wins; else if `dist²(pred, a) ≠ dist²(pred, b)` → the smaller wins; else the lower `hotspot.id` wins.

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `field(h)` | float | 0.0–1.0 | Disturbance field value at the hotspot |
| `HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` | float | 0.001–0.01 (def 0.005) | Field-equality band; **must be < `MAGNITUDE_FLOOR` (0.02)** |
| `dist²(pred, h)` | float | 0–40000 studs² | Squared predator→hotspot distance (avoids sqrt) |
| `PREDATOR_RETARGET_FIELD_MARGIN` | float | 0.07–0.15 (def **0.07**) | R4 lock-release/re-target margin; another hotspot must beat the locked field by > this; **must be > the max single-tick oscillation Δ (≈0.06, from ED ±0.03 hysteresis) AND > `HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` (0.005)** — raised from 0.05 in round-3 so one oscillation cycle cannot clear the margin |
| `PREDATOR_TARGET_DEBOUNCE_TICKS` | int | 1–4 (def 2) | Consecutive ticks a new hotspot must win before Hunt switches lock |

**Float-determinism precision contract (FIX 13):** `GetHottestHotspot` field values must be computed to **at least IEEE-754 single precision**, and the `HOTSPOT_TIE_EPSILON` band must **exceed the maximum accumulated drift per field evaluation**. This precision contract is a coordination obligation that must be **settled WITH ED before ED's interface freezes** (see Dependencies / Open Questions).

**Hunt target-change debounce (FIX 13) + margin interaction (round-3):** within Hunt, the predator does **not** switch its locked hotspot unless the new hotspot wins **by > `PREDATOR_RETARGET_FIELD_MARGIN`** for **≥ `PREDATOR_TARGET_DEBOUNCE_TICKS` consecutive ticks**. Two guards now compose: (1) the margin is set **above the max single-tick oscillation Δ** (ED ±0.03 hysteresis ⇒ Δ up to **0.06/tick** [*cross-ref: ED hysteresis knob — confirm 0.06 is current before ED interface-freeze*]), so a single oscillation cycle alone cannot clear the margin; (2) the debounce requires the win to *persist* ≥2 ticks, killing the residual case where two consecutive above-margin ticks could still flip the lock. With margin 0.05 (the prior value) a Δ0.06 cycle could clear the margin and the debounce alone was insufficient; margin 0.07 closes it.

**Output:** a single chosen hotspot, identical for identical world state (no RNG).
**Example:** field 0.713 vs 0.710 (Δ 0.003 < ε → tied) → the closer one wins; field 0.720 vs 0.710 (Δ 0.010 ≥ ε) → 0.720 wins regardless of distance.
**Extremes:** ε below 0.001 lets float-accumulation drift swap ordering between ticks (path stutter); ε above 0.01 collapses genuine "loudest wins" differences into the distance tie-break.

### D.3 — Player lock (closest alive player TO THE HOTSPOT — RR-1)

`lockTarget = argmin over { alive players within PREDATOR_SEARCH_RADIUS } of dist²(hotspot, player.HumanoidRootPart)`; ties broken by ascending `UserId`. **(RR-1: the distance is measured to the HOTSPOT, not to the predator — reconciled with Core Rule 4 and H.10, closing the round-1-surviving contradiction where this formula read `dist²(predator, player)`. Rationale: the predator commits to whoever made the loudest point.)** If the set is empty → no lock; start the Hunt→Patrol nil timer.
**Example:** hotspot at (20, 0, 10); P1 at (15, 0, 8) → `dist²(hotspot,P1) = 5²+0+2² = 29`; P2 at (10, 0, 12) → `dist² = 10²+0+2² = 104` → **lock P1** (P1 is nearer the hotspot, even though both are alive in radius).

### D.4 — BC4 knockback cap (PC H.121 re-entry guarantee)

`PREDATOR_BC4_MAX_KNOCKBACK / SPRINT_SPEED + t_react + t_latency ≤ BEACON_LINE_BREAK_GRACE`

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PREDATOR_BC4_MAX_KNOCKBACK` | float | 2–15 (def 8) studs | Max outward displacement from a hit |
| `t_react` | float | 0.15–0.30 (budget 0.25) s | Mobile reaction time; gate uses **`PREDATOR_T_REACT_MAX` = 0.30** (named constant, registry) |
| `t_latency` | float | 0.05–0.15 (budget 0.10) s | Replication round-trip; gate uses **`PREDATOR_T_LATENCY_MAX` = 0.15** (named constant, registry) |
| `BEACON_LINE_BREAK_GRACE` | float | 3 s (Crafting-owned) | Hold-zone re-entry grace |

**Example:** `8/20 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 0.75 s ≤ 3 s` ✓ (still holds if grace tightens to 1.5 s: `0.75 ≤ 1.5` ✓).
**Config-gate (FIX 5):** a startup assertion requires `BEACON_LINE_BREAK_GRACE ≥ PREDATOR_BC4_MAX_KNOCKBACK/SPRINT_SPEED + PREDATOR_T_REACT_MAX + PREDATOR_T_LATENCY_MAX` using the **named max constants** (`PREDATOR_T_REACT_MAX` = 0.30, `PREDATOR_T_LATENCY_MAX` = 0.15 — NOT the "budget" values 0.25/0.10, which are the typical-case figures used in the worked example only). At the worst-case knob box (knockback 15) this floor is `15/20 + 0.30 + 0.15 = 1.20 s`; at the default box (knockback 8) the *worked-example* re-entry is `8/20 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 0.75 s`, but the **gate always uses the max constants**, so the asserted floor is computed from the *current* knockback knob plus the fixed 0.30/0.15 maxima. This guarantees D.4 re-entry can never be unrecoverable and **raises Crafting's grace safe-range floor from 0** (cross-GDD obligation). *(The gate is `≥`; because it already uses the worst-case reaction/latency maxima, equality at the floor is the worst-case-safe bound — no additional strict-`>` margin is required.)*
**Extreme:** grace at 0 would make no hit survivable regardless of knockback — now **forbidden by the FIX-5 config-gate** (the floor is ≈1.20 s, not 0).

### D.5 — Damage / hits-to-death (never one-shots)

`damagePerHit = PLAYER_MAX_HP × DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT`; `hitsToDeath = ceil(1 / DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT)`; `effectiveDamage = min(damagePerHit, Humanoid.Health − 1)`.

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT` | float | 0.20–0.40 (def 0.30) | Fraction of max HP per hit |
| `ATTACK_COOLDOWN` | float | 2.0–4.0 (def 3.0) s | Minimum time between hit attempts |
| `PREDATOR_MELEE_RANGE` | float | 4–8 (def 6) studs | Hit range; **must be < `BEACON_HOLD_RADIUS` (12)** |
| `PLAYER_MAX_HP` | int | PC-owned (assumed 100) | **Forward obligation** — absolute damage re-derived when PC pins it |

**Output:** `hitsToDeath = ceil(1 / DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT)` hits → HP=1; `timeToDeath = (hitsToDeath − 1) × ATTACK_COOLDOWN`. At the 0.30 default: `hitsToDeath = ceil(1/0.30) = ceil(3.33) = 4`, `timeToDeath = (4 − 1) × 3.0 = 9 s` uninterrupted. **Range outcomes:** 5 hits at 0.20, 4 at 0.30, 3 at 0.40. The `Health − 1` floor makes a single hit incapable of killing a full-HP player (the no-one-shot guarantee, enforced for all game states).
**Example (0.30 default, 100 HP):** `100 → 70 → 40 → 10 → 1` (each of hits 1–3 removes 30; on hit 4 `effectiveDamage = min(rawDamage, Health − 1) = min(30, 10 − 1) = 9`, so the hit deals 9 and HP lands at 1 — the guard caps the *damage dealt* to `Health − 1`, it does NOT special-case a negative result; implement the `min(damage, Health − 1)` cap, not an `if HP − damage < 0 then HP = 1` branch) across 9 s; one 3 s sprint after any hit opens ~6 studs of net distance → clears the 6-stud melee range → breaks the chain.
**Atomic-read implementation note (FIX 20 — round-4):** `effectiveDamage = min(damage, Humanoid.Health − 1)` MUST read `Humanoid.Health` synchronously immediately before applying the cap, with **no yield between the read and the apply**. If another damage source (e.g. oxygen depletion co-landing on the same Heartbeat) drives `Health` to 0 between read and apply, `min(damage, 0 − 1) = −1` would *heal* the player by 1 — so the read-and-cap must be atomic within the same server callback.
**Extremes:** `DAMAGE_FRACTION_PER_HIT ≥ 1.0` would allow a one-shot (forbidden — config-gate rejects it); `ATTACK_COOLDOWN < 2 s` makes the post-hit reaction window too short for mobile.

## Edge Cases

- **If `GetHottestHotspot` returns nil** (no live sources, or none meet `minimumTier`): the predator falls back to Patrol behavior (ED E.5/E.5b). No error. In Hunt/Investigate this arms the 15 s nil→Patrol timeout.
- **If the locked player dies or disconnects mid-Hunt:** the lock-clear routes through **`DisturbanceService:ReleasePredatorLock`** (via DisturbanceService's `OnPlayerDied` fan-out), NOT a direct PC fire; the predator drops the lock and re-evaluates the field next tick (re-locks the new closest player **to the hotspot** if a hotspot ≥0.65 persists, else runs the nil-timer toward Patrol). On the **disconnect** path, `ReleasePredatorLock` **guards `if player and player.Parent`** before firing the `{locked = false}` clear, so the RemoteSignal is never fired against a removed `Player` object (NET-3-B1).
- **If the LAST non-locked player dies mid-Hunt (2-player → effectively solo, RR-2/R5 boundary):** the exclude-set becomes empty (only the locked player remains alive), so the quiet-check falls back to the R1 rule — it now reads the **locked player's own T**, and `effectiveQuietThreshold` uses `n = 1` (the higher small-squad threshold, default 0.45). This is an intentional mid-Hunt difficulty shift (the lone survivor must now break LOS + go quiet themselves to disengage); it is called out explicitly so it is not read as a bug. The 20 s sustain timer is NOT reset by the squadmate's death alone (only a tick `≥ effectiveQuietThreshold(1)` resets it).
- **If `GetHottestHotspot` returns a NON-nil hotspot whose `oldestContributorAge > STALE_AGE_THRESHOLD` (stale-aged but present):** the predator treats it as **nil for FSM purposes** — it does not lock/commit against a stale-aged hotspot; in Hunt/Investigate this advances the 15 s nil→Patrol timer exactly as a true nil return would. (Closes the "non-nil but stale" gap: staleness is age-gated, not only nil-gated.)
- **If `ReleasePredatorLock` is called for a player who is NOT the current lock target:** it is a **no-op** — no re-target, no spurious `PredatorEncountered`, no lock change (FIX 8).
- **If a player death races with `RunEnded` (NET-RR-1):** `DisturbanceService:ReleasePredatorLock` checks `runOutcomeResolved` first and early-returns, so no `OnPredatorLockChanged {locked = false}` fires after `runOutcomeResolved` — preserves H.2's zero-post-run-fire sentinel even when DisturbanceService's `OnPlayerDied` fan-out lands on the same Heartbeat as run-end.
- **If a player re-locks after respawning:** `PredatorEncountered` does NOT re-fire — the dedup set (`_seenPlayers`) is **session-persistent**; only the per-respawn-cleared tables (the `predatorCausedImminent` latch, its timestamp, and the current lock) reset on respawn (FIX 9).
- **If all players die (full wipe):** the predator stops, the run ends, and the instance is cleaned up with the run. No respawn-hunt.
- **If `FindPathAsync` fails or returns `NoPath`:** the predator uses **direct locomotion** toward the target position (it never freezes the Heartbeat) and retries a path on the next re-path gate. **Stuck-actor detection (FIX 11):** if direct-locomotion is active and the predator has not moved > 1 stud in 2 s, force a `FindPathAsync` retry **regardless** of `PREDATOR_REPATH_DELTA`. A **max direct-locomotion duration** (~5 s) caps the fallback — after it the predator **abandons Hunt and transitions to Patrol** even if the hotspot is still valid (FIX 20 — this is the ONE Hunt→Patrol path that fires with a *valid* hotspot, distinct from the 15 s nil→Patrol timeout; it clears the lock and the prior nav target per the state-exit cleanup rule). The destination is always Patrol (never Investigate/Disengage). Pathfinding requests are throttled by `PREDATOR_REPATH_MIN_INTERVAL` (0.5 s default; range 0.3–1.0 s) and waypoint lists carry a freshness timeout. **1-concurrent flag recovery (FIX 20):** the single-concurrent-path flag is ALWAYS cleared on `FindPathAsync` completion, error, OR a `PREDATOR_REPATH_MIN_INTERVAL`-bounded timeout (whichever comes first) — so a silently-failing or never-returning async call can never permanently latch the flag and stop the predator from re-pathing. The 2 s stuck-detection forced retry **bypasses both** the `PREDATOR_REPATH_DELTA` delta-gate and the `PREDATOR_REPATH_MIN_INTERVAL` throttle (it is an emergency unstick). A sustained unreachable target also resolves via the 5 s direct-locomotion cap (→ Patrol) and the 15 s Hunt→Patrol nil timeout.
- **If the target moves while a path is in flight:** continue following the last valid waypoint list; discard + re-path only when the target delta exceeds `PREDATOR_REPATH_DELTA` (~10 studs) and no async path is already pending (the 1-concurrent flag).
- **If two hotspots tie on field value:** resolve by squared distance, then by lowest `hotspot.id` — a total order, never random (D.2).
- **If two alive players are equidistant from the hotspot:** lock the lower `UserId` (D.3).
- **If `squadQuietT` crosses back above `effectiveQuietThreshold(n)` during the Disengage-arming window** (the n-dependent threshold, NOT a hardcoded 0.30 — at n=1 that is 0.45, at n=2 0.38, at n≥3 0.30): the 20 s sustain timer **resets** — Disengage commits only on uninterrupted quiet (ED forward-obligation #2, reset semantics; FIX 20 — the prior "0.30" was only correct at n≥3).
- **If the squad goes fully quiet during BC4:** the predator does **NOT** disengage (R2 — the quiet→Disengage path is SUPPRESSED during BC4; the beacon-commit overrides it). The only finale outcomes are **hold-and-survive** or **full-wipe** — the squad cannot win the finale by going quiet, only by holding the line and surviving the window.
- **If a louder hotspot than the beacon appears during BC4** (a player bolts and spikes the field above the `0.65` floor): the predator may briefly redirect to it — intended; breaking from the hold pulls the hunt onto the breaker (a fair, readable consequence, Pillar 2). *(At the 2-player primary squad this is sharper — the non-holder is often doing necessary work; producers should validate via Crafting BC4 that the hold/gather division does not make a 2-player BC4 a structural trap. Flagged, not resolved here — Crafting BC4 owns the win/loss balance.)*
- **If both/all holders are pinned at 1 HP during BC4 (RR-3):** the predator **cannot** kill them — `effectiveDamage = min(damage, Health − 1) = 0` at HP=1, and the no-one-shot guard holds in BC4. This is **not** an unloseable finale: the BC4 defeat condition is **oxygen depletion / the beacon-window timer (Crafting/RM-owned)**, not predator damage. The predator's pressure forces exposure that drains air faster; the air running out is the wipe. (PA owns no BC4 wipe logic — it owns only the pressure.)
- **If a hit would reduce the target to ≤0 in one blow from full or near-full HP:** `effectiveDamage = min(damage, Health − 1)` caps it — a single hit never kills (no one-shot, all states).
- **If knockback would push a player into geometry / off the navmesh:** the outward impulse is bounded (≤8 studs) and clamped to a valid navmesh position — never a wall-clip or a fling into a kill zone.
- **If the locked player outruns the predator past `PREDATOR_LOCK_RELEASE_RANGE`:** the lock releases **only if** another hotspot's field exceeds the locked hotspot's current field by > `PREDATOR_RETARGET_FIELD_MARGIN` (0.07); otherwise it does not release on range alone. **Kiting is valid but costly (R3/R4):** the lock re-evaluates every `PREDATOR_LOCK_REEVAL_INTERVAL` (~3 s), so a kiter cannot passively leash the predator while others craft — if the crafters are loud the predator switches to them; if the crafters stay quiet, nobody progresses. A quiet kiter who has outrun the predator simply loses the lock and the predator re-targets the now-loudest players.
- **If a huge hotspot appears mid-Disengage:** the predator does **not** re-enter Hunt during the committed 12 s cooldown — Disengage is a guaranteed window; it re-evaluates only after returning to Patrol.
- **If LOS to the target is blocked at melee range:** no hit lands (sight-gated) — the predator repositions for LOS rather than dealing damage through walls.
- **If the run ends (victory or wipe) while the predator is mid-path or mid-attack:** on `RunEnded` (broadcast by RunController) the predator's teardown is the **FIRST synchronous action** — it disconnects the Heartbeat and **all** event bindings **BEFORE any yield**; teardown is **idempotent**; no Predator-owned RemoteEvent/attack resolves after `runOutcomeResolved`. PredatorService also clears the `predatorCausedImminent` latch + its timestamp on `RunEnded` (FIX 9).
- **If `GetHottestHotspot` returns nil immediately after a `TierCrossedEvent(Tense)` (stale signal) while in Patrol:** the predator STAYS in Patrol — it does not transition to Investigate on a decayed/out-of-radius signal (FIX 6 nil-recovery). **Stale-aged-on-TierCrossed (FIX 20 — round-4):** the same holds if `GetHottestHotspot` returns a NON-nil but stale-aged hotspot (`oldestContributorAge > STALE_AGE_THRESHOLD`) on the back of the `TierCrossedEvent` — it is "treated as nil for FSM purposes" (H.56), so the predator STAYS in Patrol and **no timer starts** (the 15 s nil→Patrol timer is meaningless in Patrol; it only runs in Hunt/Investigate). The `TierCrossedEvent` is consumed without transition. The same re-evaluation-before-lock guard applies on Hunt entry: a nil/decayed/stale-aged hotspot starts the nil-timer rather than locking against a stale hotspot.
- **If a single solo / last-alive player goes quiet:** they can cycle the predator into Disengage repeatedly — intended; the predator is designed to be loseable by quiet play in every squad size.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**Visual identity.** The predator is a **dark void that reveals itself**, not a glowing creature — ~90% near-black warm-dark mass (`#1C1814`, Slate material), with **amber eye-shine (`#E8871A`) as the only emissive accent**. It reads first as *absence*: a horizontal silhouette mass (dorsal ridge ~4–6 player-heights, head low and forward) larger than anything the environment produces. The world's bioluminescence is the alarm; the predator stays dark against it — "the planet called it, and now it is here." First-sighting staging: silhouette-dark (scale) → amber eye-shine on advance (it sees you) → dim dorsal thermal seams at close range (biological) — never all three at once.

**Per-state visual + audio language.** State must read at lantern-edge distance (~12 studs) on an iPhone-SE-class device — movement speed, body posture, and eye-shine are the primary carriers; the flora disturbance tier is the parallel context read (the predator does not need its own state-color system because the world already communicates the same escalation).

| State | Visual read | Transition warning cue (global telegraph) | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patrol** (6) | Deliberate stalk, long settle holds, body low/horizontal; eye-shine only when facing a player | — (baseline) | Slow breathing + spaced weighted footfalls, low subsonic floor |
| **Investigate** (10) | Head lower/forward ("following a scent"), purposeful sub-walk | **Pause-and-pivot** (0.4–0.6 s freeze → turn) + audio quickening | Breathing quickens; footfall cadence rises |
| **Hunt** (16) | Low-slung lean, fast — the speed contrast IS the escalation; eye-shine tracks the locked player | **Dorsal-ridge bristle** (single-frame hackle-raise) + **sub-bass swell** | Heavy breathing + fast footfalls + subsonic pressure; **ambient/music ducks** |
| **Disengage** (6) | Drawn-back posture, moves consistently *outward* (vs. Patrol's circling); eye-shine fades to the rear | **Body-shake** (0.3 s weight-drop) + a distinct cue → signals the 12 s window has started | Footsteps receding; pressure releases — *audibly distinct* from the quieter nil-timeout |

**Sonic identity (binding):** biological/organic — breathing + footstep cadence + low subsonic presence, **non-vocalising** (never roars or screams). **Audio reach exceeds visual reach** — the predator is heard before it is seen. Audio priority: predator audio + spike alerts highest; ambient/music ducked during Hunt.

**Heard-before-seen visual redundancy (3 channels, fire before the world model is visible):**
- **A — screen-edge vignette arc** toward the predator's off-screen direction; desaturated amber, ~1.5 s pulse (0.67 Hz, well under the ≤3/s WCAG 2.3.1 ceiling), decoupled from the flora pulse; an edge arc, never a full-screen flash. **Colorblind redundancy (FIX 14):** directionality must **never rely on amber hue alone** — the arc carries a neutral light/white **stroke outline** AND/OR a distinct **pulse pattern** (shape + luminance redundancy), so a colorblind player reads direction without the hue.
- **B — directional chevron** at the screen edge (ping/waypoint grammar) once the predator is within `PREDATOR_AUDIO_RANGE` of any squad member; moves toward center and fades when the predator is on-screen.
- **C — always-on caption strip** for deaf/hard-of-hearing players (fantasy-positive — always on, not an accessibility toggle). **Full FSM + attack + lock-target vocabulary (FIX 14):** Patrol = (no caption — the state-change broadcast TO Patrol carries an explicit empty/clear caption value so the HUD zeroes the strip on Patrol entry rather than persisting a stale line); Investigate = `PRESENCE — CLOSING IN` (escapable); Hunt = `HUNTING` (committed danger, distinct from Investigate); Disengage (quiet-earned committed retreat) = `FOOTSTEPS RECEDING`; **nil-timeout → Patrol (the hotspot decayed — distinct from a quiet-earned disengage) = `PRESENCE FADING`** (round-3: the two "we lost it" outcomes MUST read differently for deaf/HoH players — Disengage is a guaranteed window, nil-timeout may immediately re-Investigate, so they carry distinct captions); attack/contact = `STRIKE` / `CONTACT`. **YOU-vs-squadmate modifier** when the viewer is the locked target: e.g. `HUNTING — YOU` vs `HUNTING — SQUAD`. **Modifier min-display (round-3):** because the lock can transfer at `PREDATOR_LOCK_REEVAL_INTERVAL` (~3 s), the YOU/SQUAD modifier holds a **minimum 0.75 s display** before changing, so a lock-transfer during pursuit cannot produce an illegible <0.5 s flicker on iPhone-SE.

**Hit / attack feedback** (must read in < 0.5 s, never a full-screen or red flash): a four-edge **vignette crush** (a **cool neutral blue-gray**, ~0.15 s onset → ~0.4 s release — deliberately *not* amber and *not* warm, so it can never read as the amber "predator" cue on a mobile display, and "amber" stays "predator," not "damage"); the **knockback motion itself** is the kinetic read (no camera shake — mobile motion-sickness concern); the hit player's **oxygen pack briefly flashes warm-white** (a squad-visible damage tell, distinct from the downed-red signal); a momentary O2-meter jerk on the victim's own HUD. The hit fully resolves in ~0.55 s, leaving a clean screen before the 3 s-cooldown next hit. **WCAG 2.3.1 (round-3):** the ~0.15 s onset is a **single, non-repeating luminance transition that does NOT return to its starting luminance within 0.067 s** (it holds, then releases over ~0.4 s), so it is **not** a "flash" under the ≤3/s definition; the `STRIKE`/`CONTACT` caption must not add a *separate* animated luminance entrance on the same frame (text swap only, no flash). The minimum 3 s `ATTACK_COOLDOWN` guarantees successive hit-vignettes are ≥3 s apart — far under the flash ceiling.

**Technical-artist handoff:** (a) eye-shine Neon LOD enable/disable with a hysteresis band so there is no pop entering lantern range — **the band must be wide enough that the amber Neon (high luminance vs the near-black mass) cannot toggle faster than 0.5 Hz** under threshold-straddling movement (round-4 WCAG, FIX 21 — UX-7: the **one-way gate per encounter is the MANDATORY approach** — enable on first lantern-range entry, disable only on full exit past `PREDATOR_AUDIO_RANGE` — which eliminates threshold-straddle toggling entirely; a plain hysteresis band is the fallback ONLY and must be **≥ `WALK_SPEED` × 2 = 24 studs** so a player walking parallel to the boundary cannot toggle it above 0.5 Hz); (b) the directional vignette-arc UIGradient updates at the ≤4 Hz HUD rate, not per-frame (iPhone-SE budget); (c) all state-transition cues are **client-side cosmetic plays** triggered by the ≤4 Hz FSM broadcast — rigged keyframe animations, never per-Part Tweens that would compete with the server locomotion driver. No custom shaders for MVP (built-in Slate / Neon only).

## UI Requirements

Predator AI owns **no standalone UI screens**. Its player-facing surfaces are HUD elements it *feeds with data*, rendered and owned by the **HUD GDD**:

1. **Directional predator indicator** — screen-edge chevron + vignette arc pointing toward the predator when it is within `PREDATOR_AUDIO_RANGE` of any squad member. The arc carries a neutral-stroke/distinct-pulse redundancy so direction never relies on amber hue alone (FIX 14).
2. **Caption strip** — always-on, full-FSM vocabulary (FIX 14 + round-3): Investigate `PRESENCE — CLOSING IN`, Hunt `HUNTING` (with the `— YOU` / `— SQUAD` modifier for the locked target, min 0.75 s display), Disengage `FOOTSTEPS RECEDING`, **nil-timeout→Patrol `PRESENCE FADING`** (distinct from Disengage), attack `STRIKE` / `CONTACT`, Patrol = cleared/empty (audio redundancy for deaf/HoH players).
3. **State-telegraph cue** — the global warning-cue surface when the predator changes state.
4. **Hit feedback** — the four-edge **cool-neutral** vignette crush + the victim's O2-meter jerk.
5. **Predator-locked-on indicator** — driven by the per-lock `OnPredatorLockChanged` RemoteSignal (Channel B), shown to the locked player only.

**Interface (RR-4 — NET-RR-2 RESOLVED; round-4 fan-out split):** Predator AI supplies two surfaces. **(1) Low-frequency state surface (broadcast, FireAll, no relevancy gating — anti-exploit):** `{ state, audioSignature, lockedPlayerId, telegraphCaption }`, fired on state/cue change and on lock transfer. **(2) High-frequency per-client sense surface (per-client targeted fire, NOT a broadcast — because `bearing` differs per recipient):** `{ bearing, distanceBand }` at **10–20 Hz** (floor 10 to avoid jitter, ceiling 20 to bound cost). `telegraphCaption` (FIX 17 — RD-3 / UX-2) is the deaf/HoH pre-state-change warning string (e.g. `CLOSING IN —`) carried on the state surface so a HoH player gets the same *anticipation* a hearing player gets from the audio swell. **Exact `worldPosition` is NO LONGER broadcast** — the payload is **bearing (per-client horizontal direction to the predator) + distanceBand (coarse `FAR`/`NEAR`/`CONTACT` enum)**. This resolves the round-1/round-3 minimap-wallhack (clients can no longer pinpoint the predator before LOS) AND preserves the heard-before-seen fantasy. `audioSignature` is a **defined 5-value enum** (`PATROL_AMBIENT` / `INVESTIGATE_QUICKEN` / `HUNT_PRESSURE` / `DISENGAGE_RECEDE` / `NIL_TIMEOUT_FADE`) selecting the client audio bed, sent **only on state/cue change** (batched with the state-change event), **never in the per-tick channel**. The per-lock `OnPredatorLockChanged` RemoteSignal targets the locked player only; the HUD renders it.
