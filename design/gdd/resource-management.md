## Overview

Resource Management owns Terranova's single survival resource for the MVP: **oxygen**, held as one **squad-shared pool** rather than per-player bars. A server-authoritative tick drains the pool continuously across the run, and the squad replenishes it by gathering and converting resources into breathable oxygen — so the pool is the run's clock, counting down toward suffocation unless the squad keeps feeding it. Because the pool is shared, every member's survival is coupled to every other's: one player's death spends the squad's oxygen, and one player's wastefulness starves the rest. RM is **infrastructure** — it owns the pool's data model, the drain and replenish math, the death-cost deduction, and the server-authoritative contracts that Player Controller, Crafting, Resource Node, and the HUD all read or write — but its **player-facing effect** is the felt pressure of a shared bar ticking down: the reason the squad must keep gathering (loud, and therefore hunted) instead of hiding quietly forever. Without Resource Management there is no survival pressure, no reason to make the noise the predator hunts, and no shared stake binding the squad together — the game would be a quiet walk with no clock.

## Player Fantasy

**The Shared Breath.**

There is one bar, and it belongs to all of you. Not four bars — one. It has been falling since you landed, slowly, the way a held breath gives out, and right now it is dipping toward red while the four of you crouch in the dark deciding what to do about it. Nobody owns a private reserve to fall back on; nobody can hand their air to a friend or hoard it from one. The only things you can do about the falling bar are the two loud, dangerous things the game keeps asking of you: *consume less* — stay still, stay quiet, don't die — or *feed it* — go out into the open and gather, which the planet hears. So you watch the number together, and you feel it as a single shared stake: your survival is not yours, it is the squad's, and the squad is watching the same bar you are.

**The clock that forces noise.** Quiet would be safe if the bar held — but it doesn't. The drain is the reason hiding is never a winning move: stay silent long enough and you suffocate in the dark with nothing hunting you at all. So someone has to stand up. You watch a teammate break cover and walk toward a node, and you *hear* the gather carry across the map, and you know the thing that hunts noise just got a fix on where you are. That is the bargain Resource Management writes into every minute of the run: the only way to keep breathing is to make the noise that gets you found. Quiet is power — but the clock means quiet, *over a full run*, runs you out.

**Honest scope (blocker 9 — two truths that are not in contradiction).** *First,* hiding is **always** a strict loss: with the Canister-only faucet, a squad that does not gather restores zero oxygen while drain continues, so a silent pool only ever falls (D.6 — net-negative at every band, every squad size). *Second,* a squad that has *already banked* a deep pool by gathering hard earlier **can** then coast the 35 s survival window in silence — the coast threshold, D.5. These do not contradict: you cannot hide your way *to* a deep pool; you can only gather (loudly) your way there, and the D.6 countdown makes arriving at the finale above the ~280 coast threshold the exception, not the rule. Going loud always buys a wider survival margin than staying silent (the ~58-unit gap between the 280 silent and 222 loud thresholds, D.5). **Squad-size truth (the modal Roblox case):** the "going loud can even *grow* the pool at the low bands" experience is a **full (3–4-player) squad** property. A **2-player squad** — the most common Roblox session — cannot out-gather even baseline drain (its concurrent-crafting cap is 1, so its faucet ≈ 0.83 u/s < BC1's 1.0): it is on a strict, unbroken countdown at *every* band, BC1 included (D.6). For two players the fantasy is purely "spend the run delaying an inevitable clock so you reach the Beacon in time," never "build a surplus." All of the above is validated at playtest (OQ.9).

**Spending the dead.** And then, at the sharpest edge, a teammate goes down — and the bar *lurches*, because their death just spent the squad's air. The deduction is automatic and final: the game doesn't ask permission and there is no separate revive transaction to haggle over (the death itself is the whole cost — CR.4/CR.5; PC brings them back on its own timer at no extra oxygen). What the squad budgets is not a revive bill but **risk** — every push into the open that might get someone killed is a push that might lurch the shared bar down by a death's worth of air, and that breath is gone for everyone, permanently within the run. No other survival game makes you feel a friend's death as a number *everyone* watches drop; this one does, with a single shared bar.

**Pillars served**
- **Pillar 2 — The Squad Is the Experience** *(primary)*: the shared pool is the most literal rendering of shared fragility — one resource, one fate; a death or a waste is everyone's loss.
- **Pillar 1 — Quiet Is Power**: the drain makes quiet *insufficient* — without it, hiding forever is optimal and the predator never matters. RM is why the squad must go loud.
- **Pillar 4 — Rounds, Not Saves**: within a run, oxygen spent is gone and a death's cost is permanent; the bar only goes one way unless you earn it back.

**What the player should feel** — that they are breathing on borrowed, shared air, and that every quiet minute is also a falling one. Not the power-fantasy of a full tank, but the intimacy and dread of a single number four people are keeping alive together — and the knowledge that the only way to keep it up is to risk being heard.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

**CR.1 — Pool data model.** Oxygen is a single server-side scalar `P`, owned exclusively by `ResourceService` (Knit Service, server-only), clamped to `[0, OXYGEN_POOL_START]`. It is **one undifferentiated squad-shared resource**: there are no per-player bars, no private reserves, and no mechanism to hoard, gift, or allocate oxygen between members. The only levers any player has are *consume-less* (stay alive, avoid the deaths that spend it) and *feed-more* (gather → craft → use Canisters). Clients receive a **read-only**, server-authoritative projection; they never hold a write handle to `P` and never mutate it. To keep the bar smooth under the high finale drain, the client **dead-reckons**: it renders `P_displayed = P_lastSync − drainRate_lastSync × timeSinceSync` (extrapolating downward at the last-synced band drain), snapping to the authoritative value on every sync. Display tolerance is therefore **±0.5 units of the dead-reckoned estimate**, *except* within one sync interval after an un-forecast discrete event (a Canister restore or a death deduction the client has not yet received), which resolves on the next sync. The sync payload carries both `P` **and** the current `drainRate` (band) so the client can dead-reckon (RBI-4 fix — at 8.0 u/s a naïve ±0.5 bound is unachievable between 2 Hz syncs: the bar would be up to 4 units stale).

**Dead-reckon base reset on discrete events (blocker 7).** The two discrete semantic events — `OnOxygenDeducted(amount, reason, simultaneousCount, P_after)` and `OnOxygenRestored(amount, source, P_after)` — each carry the **authoritative post-event pool `P_after`** (the server's `P` immediately after that event's CR.3 sub-step resolved this tick). On receipt the client **resets its dead-reckon base** to `P_after` and re-anchors the downward extrapolation from there, *without waiting for the next periodic sync*.

**`P_after` is pinned to a specific CR.3 sub-step (blocker 2 — removes the round-3 "post-deductions/restores" ambiguity).** Both events carry a *pre-continuous-drain* snapshot, but at **different** sub-steps of the fixed per-tick order (CR.3: ① deductions → ② restores → ③ drain):
- `OnOxygenDeducted.P_after` = `P` **after step ① (all death deductions), before step ② (restores) and step ③ (drain)**.
- `OnOxygenRestored.P_after` = `P` **after step ② (all restores), before step ③ (drain)**.

RM **emits the two events in sub-step order — `OnOxygenDeducted` first, then `OnOxygenRestored`** — so that *in the common in-order case* the client re-bases sequentially (to the deduction snapshot, then the restore snapshot) and lands on the step-② value, which matches the server's pre-drain `P`; it then resumes extrapolating the continuous drain (step ③) downward from there. **Network-ordering caveat (round-4, network gate N2):** Roblox does **not** guarantee cross-Signal delivery order — under jitter a client could receive the restore event before the deduction event and momentarily re-base to the lower deduction snapshot. The **authoritative correction is the periodic `OnOxygenChanged` sync** (CR.1): any same-tick reorder self-heals within one sync interval (≤ 0.5 s at 2 Hz). Crucially, **the Empty *state cue* is driven ONLY by the authoritative `OnOxygenStateChanged("Empty")` server event — never by the dead-reckoned bar reaching 0** — so a reorder can at worst cause a brief bar-*position* transient, never a false Empty alarm/red-pulse. (The sub-step emission order is therefore a best-effort display nicety, not a correctness guarantee; correctness rests on the periodic sync + the authoritative state cue.) This rule resolves three otherwise-separate visual defects together: (1) the death **lurch-down** lands on the correct value immediately instead of drifting until the next 2 Hz sync; (2) when a discrete event and the same-tick continuous drain coalesce, the client never double-applies drain (it re-bases to the pinned pre-drain `P_after` and resumes extrapolating step-③ drain from there); and (3) a band change that arrives via the next `OnOxygenChanged` sync carries the fresh `drainRate`, so the BC2→BC3 transition no longer accumulates the ~2.9 u dead-reckon error the round-2 review flagged. `P_after` is **display-only**; it never grants the client a write handle to the server `P` (the spine is unchanged, H.1/H.29).

**Latency-pop bound at BC4 (IMPORTANT).** Because `P_after` is a server snapshot, by the time the client receives and re-bases on it the server `P` has already fallen by `latency × drainRate`; at BC4 (`8.0 u/s`) a 100 ms one-way latency makes the re-base land ~0.8 u high — a visible "pop" exceeding the ±0.5 tolerance on a poor mobile connection. To bound it, each discrete event **also carries the server send-timestamp** (`serverSendTime`, `workspace:GetServerTimeNow()`), and the client projects the snapshot forward by `(clientReceiveTime − serverSendTime) × drainRate_band` before adopting it as the new base — so the pop is reduced to the residual clock-skew error, not the full latency × drain. (The projection is display-only; it never writes server `P`.)

**CR.2 — Per-band drain schedule.** Drain is continuous and **ramps with the current escalation band (Beacon Charge Tier, BC1→BC4)**, flat within a band, and **independent of how many players are alive** (flat squad drain). Unit convention: `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC1 = 1.0` unit/s *by definition* (1 oxygen unit = 1 second of BC1 drain).

| Band | Constant | Value (units/s) |
|---|---|---|
| BC1 (baseline) | `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC1` | 1.0 |
| BC2 | `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC2` | 1.5 |
| BC3 | `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC3` | 2.2 |
| BC4 (Beacon survival window) | `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR` | 8.0 |

RM **reads** the current band from the escalation authority each tick; it owns no band state (see Interactions). BC4 is specifically the Beacon survival window, and `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR` is the value Crafting H.106 references. **Round-2 change:** BC4 drain was raised 3.5 → 8.0 when the CR.9 BC4-entry oxygen cap was removed (user ruling, RBI-2) — the steep BC4 drain is now the **sole** shared-pool anti-bunker fence (a silent, zero-Canister squad survives the 35 s window only if it enters with ≥ 280 oxygen; see D.5). The `_FLOOR` suffix is historical: this is now a flat BC4 drain, tunable upward, not a floor beneath a removed cap.

**CR.3 — Per-tick processing order (60 Hz, normative).** Every `RunService.Heartbeat` tick, server-side, RM applies the tick's deltas in a **fixed deterministic order**, clamping to `[0, OXYGEN_POOL_START]` after each step:
1. **All death deductions** queued this tick (CR.4), each idempotent per `(userId, deathEventId)`;
2. **All Canister restores** queued this tick (CR.6);
3. **The once-per-tick continuous drain:** `P ← max(0, P − drainRate(band) × dtₑ)`, where `dtₑ = min(max(dt, 0), MAX_TICK_DT)` — the Heartbeat delta, guarded non-negative (negative-`dt` guard, same clock-seam discipline as Crafting) **and capped at `MAX_TICK_DT = 0.1 s` (IMPORTANT)** so a lag/GC spike or a resumed-from-suspend frame cannot crater the pool with one giant catch-up drain (at BC4 an uncapped 2 s hitch would drain 16 units in a single tick). `drainRate(band)` maps the current Beacon Charge Tier to the CR.2 table, read at tick start (CR.2).

The **Empty** state and the `OnPlayerOxygenExpired` cascade are evaluated on the **final `P`** after all three steps, so a same-tick restore that lifts `P > 0` legitimately prevents the cascade (the "last-moment save" edge). No queued delta is ever dropped. This per-tick order is the normative source; the matching edge case and H.43 restate it. After the tick, RM emits the discrete semantic events **in sub-step order — `OnOxygenDeducted` first (carrying `P_after` = the pool after step ①), then `OnOxygenRestored` (carrying `P_after` = the pool after step ②)** — for client re-basing (CR.1, blocker 2/7). The step-① death deductions are applied **idempotently per `(userId, deathEventId)`** (CR.4); when N deaths coalesce in one tick, only the non-duplicate keys apply, and the single coalesced `OnOxygenDeducted` carries `amount` = the *actually-applied* total and `simultaneousCount` = the count of *applied* deductions (blocker 6, H.44).

**CR.4 — Death-cost deduction.** On `RequestSquadOxygenSpend(amount, reason, deathEventId)` (server-internal Knit call, **never a RemoteEvent** — no client trust surface): for `reason = "death"`, deduct `amount × DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` (normally `amount = 1` ⇒ `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST = 72`), clamped at 0. **Idempotent** per `(userId, deathEventId)`: a duplicate `deathEventId` for the same player is a no-op. `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` is RM-owned and satisfies DP-2 (`DEATH_OXYGEN_COST / OXYGEN_POOL_START ∈ [0.10, 0.15]`). The `(userId, deathEventId)` dedup set lives in RM's run-session state and is cleared at run end; `deathEventId` is PC-owned (the cross-service idempotency key). **Two idempotency contracts (IMPORTANT):** (1) **PC guarantees `deathEventId` is unique for the lifetime of the run session** (session-lifetime-monotonic — never reused across deaths *or* across runs within a session), so the `(userId, deathEventId)` key cannot collide; (2) RM **writes the dedup key into the set BEFORE performing the deduction** (write-before-throwable-work), so that if any later step in the same call throws, a replication retry sees the key already present and is a clean no-op — a partially-applied death can never be double-charged on retry. The dedup set is cleared on run end (CR-run-cleanup); a fresh run starts with an empty set. **Run-end race guard (IMPORTANT; tick-ordering pinned, network gate N1):** `RequestSquadOxygenSpend` carries a **run-state check** — once the run has entered its ended/resolved state, an in-flight or late-arriving spend is **rejected as a no-op** (it neither charges the pool nor writes the dedup set), so a spend cannot re-populate the set *after* the run-end clear and leak into the next run. **The run-end state flip is processed at END-of-tick — after step ① deductions, steps ②/③, and the Empty/outcome evaluation of that tick.** So a death **queued for the final tick still charges in step ①** (attribution preserved for a last-tick death); only spends arriving on a *subsequent* tick (after the flip) are rejected. The dedup-set clear (H.53) runs as part of the same end-of-tick run-end processing, after the guard is armed, so no in-tick death is dropped and no post-flip spend re-populates the set — run-end is deterministic regardless of spend arrival timing.

**Idempotency × coalescing (blocker 6 — reconciles CR.4 with H.44).** When **N ≥ 1** deaths are queued in the same tick (step ① of CR.3), RM applies each **independently and idempotently per its own `(userId, deathEventId)`**: a key already in the dedup set is a no-op and contributes **nothing** to the tick's deduction. Let **k ≤ N** be the count of *non-duplicate* keys actually applied this tick. Then: (1) the pool is reduced by `k × DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` (clamped at 0), never `N ×` — a duplicate cannot be double-charged even inside a coalesced batch; (2) RM emits the coalesced `OnOxygenDeducted` **once** with `amount = k × DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` (the *applied* total, not the requested total) and `simultaneousCount = k` (the *applied* count, so the HUD renders k coalesced cues, not N); (3) if `k = 0` (every queued death was a duplicate), RM applies nothing and emits **no** `OnOxygenDeducted` event at all. The event's `P_after` (CR.1, blocker 2) is the post-step-① pool, i.e. after the `k × DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` reduction.

**CR.5 — Respawn.** There is no separate respawn deduction — the CR.4 death cost is the entire cost of a death. PC owns the `RESPAWN_DELAY = 30 s` timer; RM owns only the pool math.

**CR.6 — Canister restore (the single faucet).** On `OnOxygenPulseRequest(amount, requestingPlayerId)` (fire-and-forget server-internal Knit **`Signal`** — not a `RemoteSignal`, no client trust surface — from Crafting's `RequestUseItem("OxygenCanister")`): `P ← min(OXYGEN_POOL_START, P + CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN)`. **RM conforms to Crafting's published signature** (the caller owns the contract, per the round-1 CD ruling). **RM owns the restore math**: *both* the `amount` and `requestingPlayerId` parameters are audit/logging only and are *not* trusted for the calculation — RM always restores its own `CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN` constant (prevents a compromised or spoofed event from over-restoring). *(Forward-pending reconciliation, RBI-1: Crafting is internally inconsistent — it publishes `OnOxygenPulseRequest` at crafting-and-items.md:364 + 4 sites but names it `OnOxygenRestoreRequested` once at line 938. RM tracks the published-majority name `OnOxygenPulseRequest`; Crafting's next revision must converge on the single name.)* By construction `CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN (10) < DEATH_OXYGEN_COST (72)` — a Canister can never refund a death. **This is the only oxygen faucet**: there is no passive regeneration and no direct gather-to-oxygen path. Gather nodes yield crafting materials; oxygen exists only after BIOMASS → Canister → use.

**CR.7 — Bounds.** Floor `P ≥ 0` (reaching 0 enters Empty, see States). Ceiling `P ≤ OXYGEN_POOL_START` (Canister restores clamp here; no overfill). Oxygen is never negative and never exceeds its starting value during a run.

**CR.8 — Anti-idle invariant (DEG-1). ⚠ JOINTLY-OWNED cross-GDD invariant (seam-patch 2026-07-05, cross-review W2):** this rule is the game's ONLY mechanical closure of the full-passive-camping strategy — ED's disturbance model alone permits a permanent-Calm attractor (ED H.PB1-BETA criterion (iv) instruments it, but nothing in ED *prevents* it). **Do not retune `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC1..BC4` toward zero-equivalent (or add a passive regen faucet) without re-verifying against ED's Calm-attractor risk in the same patch session** — the anti-camping guarantee lives in the RM×ED composition, not in either GDD alone. (ED carries the mirror note at its G.7 knob-interaction warnings.) Because the faucet is Canister-only, a squad that hides and does not gather restores **zero** oxygen while drain continues — net oxygen is strictly negative at **every** band. Hiding is therefore always a losing stall. Active play replenishes through a **bench-bottlenecked** faucet (Crafting caps concurrent crafting at `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP = min(2, ceil(squad/2))` — gathering parallelizes across all members, but turning BIOMASS into Canisters does not), so squad throughput is bounded well below the top-band drains. **Squad-size caveat (blocker 8):** the cap is **2 only for a 3–4-player squad**; a **2-player squad has `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP = min(2, ceil(2/2)) = 1`**, so its sustained faucet (`≈ 0.83 u/s` at `canisterCycleTime ≈ 12 s`) is **below even BC1 drain (1.0)** — a 2-player squad is net-negative at *every* band, including BC1. The "can sustain or slightly grow the pool at the low bands" property therefore holds **only for a full (3–4-player) squad committing everyone to loud gathering**; at BC3 and especially BC4 even a full squad's maximum throughput is net-negative. The run therefore always trends toward a forced, loud finale — quiet never sustains the pool at any band, and no amount of gathering stops the clock once escalation reaches the top bands (full derivation, with the per-squad-size table, in D.6).

### States and Transitions

The pool has one **squad-wide** state (not per-player). Thresholds use a 2-unit hysteresis on recovery to prevent `OnOxygenStateChanged` flapping near the boundary.

| State | Entry condition | Exits to | Server event on entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Healthy** | `P > OXYGEN_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD (35)` | → Critical when `P ≤ 35` | `OnOxygenStateChanged("Healthy")` (fires from Critical only when `P > 35 + 2`) |
| **Critical** | `0 < P ≤ 35` | → Healthy when `P > 37`; → Empty when `P = 0` | `OnOxygenStateChanged("Critical")` (HUD red-warning) |
| **Empty** | `P = 0` (after tick clamp) | → **Critical** (or Healthy) only if a Canister restore lands the same frame and lifts `P > 0` | `OnOxygenStateChanged("Empty")` → `OnPlayerOxygenExpired()` **one argument-less squad-wide broadcast** (see arity note below) |

**Empty → death cascade.** On entering Empty, RM fires `OnPlayerOxygenExpired()` **exactly once** (edge-triggered on the Empty *entry* transition, *not* every tick while `P = 0`) as a single squad-wide broadcast; PC consumes it and runs its oxygen-empty death path + grace timer for every currently-alive player (PC-owned). The pool stays at 0 and further drain ticks are no-ops. A Canister restore that arrives in the same server frame `P` reaches 0 is still applied (CR.6) — a last-moment refuel can pull the squad out of Empty before the death path resolves; a single Canister yields `P = 10`, so the squad re-enters **Critical** (not Healthy). **Grace-timer ownership RESOLVED (cross-GDD seam patch 2026-07-05, cross-review C4; USER-RULED: PC owns, 5 s):** this paragraph's "(PC-owned)" was correct — PC's contrary attribution has been fixed. The pinned contract: PC starts ONE squad-wide `OXYGEN_GRACE_DURATION = 5 s` timer (PC G.6) on this broadcast; at expiry PC re-checks the pool via RM's server-internal pool read and applies the death path only if still `P = 0` — a mid-grace Canister restore (CR.6) cancels the pending deaths; a fresh Empty entry re-fires this broadcast and restarts the timer. RM's only obligations: this edge-triggered broadcast (unchanged) + the server-internal read-only pool accessor for PC's expiry re-check.

**Arity note (IMPORTANT — resolves the round-2 singular-vs-plural ambiguity).** When the *shared* pool empties, **the whole squad suffocates simultaneously** — there is no single "expired player." So `OnPlayerOxygenExpired()` takes **no `playerId` argument**: it is one squad-wide signal, and PC applies the oxygen-empty death to every alive player on receipt. The round-2 "`(playerId)`" signature was a vestige of a per-player model that does not fit the shared pool. *(PC reconciliation obligation, logged OQ.7: PC's F.2 RM row must drop the `playerId` parameter on its next revision; RM's contract is the argument-less squad-wide form. This is the shared-pool-correct arity, not a re-opening of the death-cost contract, which is unchanged.)*

## Formulas

All math is in RM oxygen units (1 unit = 1 second of BC1 drain). Candidate constant values are shown here and listed as tunable in Tuning Knobs.

**D.1 — Continuous drain (per Heartbeat tick)**

`P_next = clamp(P − drainRate(BCT) × dt, 0, OXYGEN_POOL_START)`

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P` | float | [0, 600] | Current shared pool |
| `drainRate(BCT)` | float | {1.0, 1.5, 2.2, 8.0} | Drain for current Beacon Charge Tier (CR.2) |
| `dt` | float | clamped to `[0, MAX_TICK_DT]`, `MAX_TICK_DT = 0.1` | Heartbeat delta, guarded non-negative **and** capped (CR.3) so a lag/GC spike can't crater the pool |
| `OXYGEN_POOL_START` | float | 600 (tunable) | Pool ceiling = run-start value |

**Output range:** [0, 600], clamped both ends. **Example:** `P = 400`, BC3, `dt = 1/60` → `400 − 2.2 × 0.01667 = 399.963`.

**D.2 — Canister restore (the single faucet)**

`P_next = clamp(P + CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN, 0, OXYGEN_POOL_START)`

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN` | float | 10 (tunable, `< DEATH_OXYGEN_COST`) | Units restored per Canister use |

**Output range:** [0, 600]. **Example:** `P = 50` → `min(600, 50 + 10) = 60`.

**D.3 — Death deduction**

`P_next = clamp(P − amount × DEATH_OXYGEN_COST, 0, OXYGEN_POOL_START)`

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | int | ≥ 1 (normally 1) | Death-count multiplier from PC's spend call |
| `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` | float | 72 (RM-owned, tunable within the DP-2 band) | Units per death |

**Output range:** [0, 600]. **Example:** `P = 300`, `amount = 1` → `300 − 72 = 228`.

**D.4 — DP-2 death-cost ratio invariant** *(config-validation, carried from PC H.87; asserted fail-fast at server init)*

`DEATH_OXYGEN_COST / OXYGEN_POOL_START ∈ [0.10, 0.15]`

**Evaluated:** `72 / 600 = 0.12` ✓. Plus the dependent invariant `CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN < DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` → `10 < 72` ✓.

**D.5 — BC4 anti-bunker fence** *(restated in shared-pool terms — the CR.9 entry cap was removed, RBI-2; the raised BC4 drain is now the sole fence)*

With CR.9 gone, the bound on oxygen-at-window-entry is no longer a fixed cap — it is whatever pool the squad carries in, which the countdown backbone (D.6) drives down over the run. The fence is now two properties of the BC4 drain:

- **(a) Over-supply — HARD init assertion** (the squad's *entire* Canister supply, by itself, cannot cover the window's drain):
  `ITEM_STACK_MAX × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN < BEACON_SURVIVAL_WINDOW_min × OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR`

  **The left side counts ONE squad stack — and that is only sound under a per-squad cap (blocker 1, CD/USER ruling R3-3).** As written, `ITEM_STACK_MAX × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN = 10 × 10 = 100` assumes the squad's banked Canister supply is bounded by **one** `ITEM_STACK_MAX` stack. If Canisters were instead capped **per member**, a 4-player squad could bank `4 × 10 × 10 = 400 > 280` and the over-supply fence would be **false** — the squad could carry enough banked Canisters to out-restore the entire window. So this inequality is only honest if Canister supply is **squad-bounded, not per-member.** **R3-3 (user ruling) resolves it: a per-squad Canister-stack cap** — the squad as a whole holds at most one effective `ITEM_STACK_MAX` stack of Canisters, so `100 < 280` holds and the protected `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR = 8.0` is untouched. RM must **lock `ITEM_STACK_MAX`'s per-squad semantics with Crafting** (Crafting owns the inventory/stack model; a new cross-GDD obligation is logged at **OQ.10**). H.26 asserts `100 < 280` at init and is therefore conditional on that per-squad cap being confirmed.

  **Reconciliation with Crafting H.106 (round-2 blocker 2, CD ruling R2-1):** Crafting H.106's binding form carries an additive `oxygen_pool_start` term on the left (a per-member starting reserve). RM's form **intentionally drops that term.** Under the shared pool there is *no* per-member entry reserve to add — there is one squad pool, and the question "how much oxygen does the squad bring into the window?" is not a fixed per-member constant but the run-driven entry pool, which is governed by RM's **coast threshold** (below) instead. So the over-supply assertion is correctly an *item-supply* bound (`stack × restore < window × drain`), and the entry-reserve question is carried separately by the coast threshold — not folded into this inequality. Crafting H.106 reconciles to this shared-pool form on its next revision (logged OQ.8(iii)); R2-1 confirms RM's form governs.
- **(b) Always-a-countdown at the finale** (sustained squad faucet throughput is strictly below BC4 drain — *partly forward-pending on RN's `canisterCycleTime`*):
  `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN / canisterCycleTime < OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR`

| Variable | Source | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `ITEM_STACK_MAX` | Crafting (owns) | 10 |
| `BEACON_SURVIVAL_WINDOW_min` | Crafting (owns) | 35 s |
| `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP` | Crafting (owns, = `min(2, ceil(squad/2))`) | 2 (full squad) |
| `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR` | RM | 8.0 |

**Evaluated:** (a) `10 × 10 = 100 < 35 × 8.0 = 280` ✓ (margin 180 — far healthier than the old CR.9 7.5; conditional on the R3-3 per-squad cap, above). (b) `2 × 10 / ~12 ≈ 1.67 < 8.0` ✓. The BC4 leg holds for any `canisterCycleTime > 2.5 s` (strict), but that is **not** the binding floor: the *backbone* constraint is the much tighter **HARD `canisterCycleTime ≥ ~9.1 s`** floor that keeps **BC3** net-negative for a full squad (D.6) — anything in `[2.5, 9.1) s` satisfies (b) yet flips BC3 net-positive and breaks "always a countdown," so 9.1 s governs.

**Coast threshold (the residual the user accepted by removing CR.9):** a **silent**, zero-Canister squad survives the 35 s window iff it enters with `P ≥ BEACON_SURVIVAL_WINDOW_min × OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR = 280` (entering at exactly 280 **survives** — the `≥` tie resolves in the squad's favour, the Beacon-survival check winning the final-tick tie over oxygen-empty, CD ruling R3-2; see H.49). With **active** play (full-squad restore ≈ 1.67 u/s) the break-even entry pool is `35 × (8.0 − 1.67) ≈ 222`.

**Honest framing — arithmetic, not assertion (blocker 1/9 — replaces the round-3 circular "bounded by the coast threshold" dismissal).** The fence is a *probability shaped by the countdown*, NOT an absolute guarantee, and the honest question is **"how does a squad reach a 280+ entry pool, and how hard is it?"** Two contributions, summed:
- **Banked Canisters.** Under the per-squad cap (R3-3), the squad's *entire* banked Canister supply is at most one stack = `ITEM_STACK_MAX × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN = 100` units. So banked items alone contribute **at most 100 of the needed 280** — they cannot, by themselves, fund a coast. (Without the R3-3 cap, per-member banking would reach `400 > 280` and *would* fund a silent coast outright — which is exactly why R3-3 is load-bearing, not cosmetic.)
- **Carried pool.** The remaining `≥ 180` units must be a *deep carried pool* — but the D.6 countdown actively opposes carrying one: a full squad is net-negative from BC3 on (≈ −0.53 u/s through the ~120 s BC3 phase ⇒ it *loses* ~64 units across BC3 alone), and a 2-player squad is net-negative at **every** band. To enter BC4 at 280 a full squad must therefore *exit BC2 well above 280* and then bleed down through BC3 — achievable only by a squad that gathered hard and took no deaths, and even then the BIOMASS-allocation tension (every Canister crafted is a Beacon material not crafted) pulls the achievable pool lower.

So coasting is **possible but the minority outcome**: banked items max out at 100 (< 280), the other 180+ demands a deep pool the countdown is constantly draining, and going loud always buys the ~58-unit-wider margin (280 silent vs 222 loud). "The squad never coasts" overstates it; the honest claim is "coasting requires arriving deep *and* lucky, banked Canisters can't fund it alone, and loud play always widens the margin." Exact coast frequency is a **playtest question (OQ.9, raised in priority)** — if silent coasting proves common, `OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC4_FLOOR` raises toward 12.0.

**D.6 — Net-oxygen rate / anti-idle (DEG-1), squad-total** *(RBI-3 — restated as an explicit squad-total invariant; the round-1 form mis-treated a per-gatherer rate as squad-total)*

`dP/dt = restoreRate_squad − drainRate(BCT)`, where
`restoreRate_squad = min(N_gatherers, CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP) × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN / canisterCycleTime` (and `= 0` when the squad does not gather).

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `N_gatherers` | int | 1–4 | Squad members actively running the gather→craft→use loop |
| `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP` | int | 2 (full squad; `= min(2, ceil(squad/2))`, Crafting-owned) | Concurrent-crafting bottleneck — gathering parallelizes, Canister crafting does not |
| `canisterCycleTime` | float | ~10–14 s (RN-dependent — see note) | Best-case gather→craft→use cycle per Canister |
| `restoreRate_squad` | float | 0 hiding; ≈ 1.5–1.7 units/s at full loud play | Bench-bottlenecked sustained faucet throughput |

**Why the cap is the backbone (RBI-3 fix):** the round-1 doc treated `restoreRate ≈ 0.7–1.0 u/s` as squad-total; in fact it is *per-gatherer*, and four independent gatherers (≈ 3.3 u/s) would out-pace BC3's 2.2 → a net-positive pool, breaking "always a countdown." The real ceiling is Crafting's **concurrent-crafting cap of 2** for a full squad: no matter how many members gather, only two Canisters are produced at a time, so `restoreRate_squad ≈ 2 × (10 / ~12) ≈ 1.67 u/s`.

**DEG-1 result (band-by-band, full loud play) — by squad size (blocker 8).** `dP/dt` depends on `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP = min(2, ceil(squad/2))`, which is **1 for a 2-player squad** and **2 for a 3–4-player squad**. At the provisional `canisterCycleTime ≈ 12 s`, `restoreRate_squad = cap × 10 / 12` ⇒ **≈ 0.83 u/s (2-player)** vs **≈ 1.67 u/s (3–4-player)**:

| Band (drain) | 2-player `dP/dt` (cap 1, ≈0.83) | 3–4-player `dP/dt` (cap 2, ≈1.67) |
|---|---|---|
| Hiding (0) | −drain (net-negative every band) | −drain (net-negative every band) |
| BC1 (1.0) | **≈ −0.17** (net-negative even at BC1) | ≈ +0.67 (can grow, but maximally loud) |
| BC2 (1.5) | ≈ −0.67 | ≈ +0.17 (roughly sustainable, loud) |
| BC3 (2.2) | ≈ −1.37 | ≈ −0.53 *(conditional — see below)* |
| BC4 (8.0) | ≈ −7.17 | ≈ −6.33 (hard finale clock) |

- **Hiding** (`restoreRate_squad = 0`): `dP/dt = −drainRate < 0` at **every** band, every squad size — hiding is always net-negative (rock-solid, structural).
- **2-player:** net-negative at **every** band including BC1 (one crafter can't outrun even baseline drain). The "+grow at low bands" experience is a **full-squad property only**; a 2-player squad is always on a countdown. (Honest difficulty, intended under the flat-drain ruling — OQ.3/OQ.4; 2-player is modal on Roblox, so this is the common-case experience, not an edge.)
- **3–4-player:** can sustain or slightly grow the pool at BC1/BC2 *only by committing everyone to loud gathering* (Pillar 1: maximally hunted); net-negative at BC3/BC4.

**BC3 net-negativity is conditional, not structural (blocker 4, forward-pending — like H.23 for BC4).** For a full squad, BC3 is net-negative **iff** `restoreRate_squad < OXYGEN_DRAIN_BC3`, i.e. `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP × CANISTER_RESTORE_OXYGEN / canisterCycleTime < 2.2` ⇒ **`canisterCycleTime > 20 / 2.2 ≈ 9.1 s`**. At the provisional 12 s it holds (≈ −0.53), but a faster cycle would flip BC3 net-positive and break "always trends toward the finale." This cannot be asserted as a structural fact until Resource Node authors `BIOMASS_GATHER_TIME` (hence H.23-style forward-pending). **HARD requirement on Resource Node / Level (not a soft advisory):** `canisterCycleTime ≥ ~9.1 s`. The plausible RN output band (`~[7.5, 11.1] s`) *overlaps the BC3-breaking zone* (anything `< 9.1 s` flips BC3 positive) — precisely why this floor is HARD and must be re-validated the moment `BIOMASS_GATHER_TIME` is authored. (An upper bound `~13 s` keeps BC1 only *marginally* positive for a full squad; see the Note below.)

**Restated invariant:** the run **always trends toward a forced, loud finale**. A *full* squad may sustain or slightly grow the pool at the low bands *only by being maximally loud*; a 2-player squad is net-negative at every band; and once escalation reaches BC4 no throughput at any squad size stops the clock. Quiet never sustains the pool at any band.

**BIOMASS allocation tension (IMPORTANT, folded in):** `restoreRate_squad` is an *upper bound* — in practice the squad must also divert BIOMASS to **Beacon materials** (Crafting's win-condition), so real sustained restore is lower than 1.67 u/s. Canisters-for-survival vs Beacon-materials-for-winning is a live strategic tension that makes the countdown bite harder than the raw faucet math implies. The **exact diversion ratio is not quantified here** because it depends on Crafting's Beacon material cost + RN's `BIOMASS_GATHER_TIME` (both unauthored/forward-pending); RM treats the unquantified diversion as *additional* downward pressure on the effective faucet (it only ever *lowers* `restoreRate_squad` below the 1.67 u/s ceiling, so the "always a countdown" backbone is conservative against it). Quantifying it is a balance-pass OQ once Crafting's Beacon cost lands (tracked under OQ.6/OQ.9).

**Note:** `canisterCycleTime` depends on BIOMASS gather time, owned by the unauthored Resource Node GDD (provisional ~8 s/gather) — forward dependency. **HARD constraint on RN/Level (forward-pending, blocker 4):** `canisterCycleTime ≥ ~9.1 s` so full-squad BC3 stays net-negative (the backbone); an upper *soft* bound `~13 s` keeps BC1 only *marginally* positive for a full squad (a too-fast cycle lets the squad bank oxygen trivially at low bands; a too-slow one makes even BC1 unwinnable). At `canisterCycleTime ≈ 12 s` both hold; RN **must re-validate the ≥ 9.1 s floor** once `BIOMASS_GATHER_TIME` is authored — its plausible output band `~[7.5, 11.1] s` overlaps the breaking zone.

**Worked run example (the countdown — total-run balance, NOT in-window):** a representative 600 s run at 180 s BC1 / 240 s BC2 / 120 s BC3 / 60 s BC4 drains `180·1.0 + 240·1.5 + 120·2.2 + 60·8.0 = 180 + 360 + 264 + 480 = 1284` units. Pool (600) plus Canister restores must cover the rest (~684 units ≈ 68 Canisters' worth). **This ~68-Canister figure is spread across the ENTIRE ~600 s run, not the BC4 window** — crafting 68 Canisters during the 60 s (let alone 35 s) finale is temporally impossible at `canisterCycleTime ≈ 12 s` / cap 2 (≈ 10 Canisters max in 60 s). The BC4 portion (480 units over 60 s) is therefore covered almost entirely by the **pool the squad carries into the finale**, not by in-window crafting — which is exactly why the entry pool (and the D.5 coast threshold) governs the finale, and why the squad must bank oxygen *before* BC4 by gathering continuously through BC1–BC3, and never by hiding.

## Edge Cases

- **If `P` reaches 0 (Empty):** RM fires `OnPlayerOxygenExpired` squad-wide; the pool holds at 0; subsequent drain ticks are no-ops. PC owns the resulting deaths.
- **If a Canister restore lands the same server frame `P` hits 0:** the restore is still applied (CR.6); if it lifts `P > 0` the squad exits Empty before PC's death path resolves — a legitimate last-moment save (a single Canister yields `P = 10` ⇒ the squad re-enters **Critical**, not Healthy).
- **If a Canister restore would push `P` above `OXYGEN_POOL_START`:** clamp at 600; the overflow is lost. Oxygen cannot be banked above the run-start value — there is no stockpiling *inside the pool* (only as un-used Canister items, which Crafting caps at `ITEM_STACK_MAX`).
- **If a duplicate death spend arrives (same `(userId, deathEventId)`):** no-op (idempotent, CR.4). Pathological replication double-fires cannot double-charge a death.
- **If a death spend arrives when `P ≤ DEATH_OXYGEN_COST`:** deduct and clamp at 0, which triggers the Empty cascade. A death can be the event that empties the pool.
- **If `dt ≤ 0` on a Heartbeat tick (clock step / NTP correction / VM migration):** the `max(0, …)` guard means that tick applies **no** drain and can **never** add oxygen. Drain only ever reduces `P`.
- **If the Beacon-Charge-Tier accessor is unavailable / `nil`** (ED/Beacon not yet initialized, or a transient read failure): RM defaults to **BC1 (slowest drain)** for that tick and logs — fail-safe; never stalls the pool and never over-drains on missing data. *(Forward-pending until the ED/Beacon accessor is finalized.)*
- **If the squad has no reachable bench or no BIOMASS** (the Canister-only consequence): the squad **cannot** replenish at all and the pool drains to 0 → loss. This is intended (the bench + materials are a survival dependency), but it imposes a **level-design constraint**: every map MUST guarantee reachable BIOMASS nodes and bench access, or the run is unwinnable by construction. *(Flagged to Resource Node + Level design.)*
- **If a player disconnects mid-run (`PlayerRemoving`, not a death):** **no** oxygen is charged (a disconnect is not `reason="death"`), and because drain is flat-headcount the rate is unchanged — the squad simply loses that player's gather contribution.
- **If a death and a Canister restore resolve in the same tick:** RM applies queued discrete deltas in a **fixed deterministic order** — (1) all death deductions, (2) all Canister restores, (3) the once-per-tick continuous drain — clamping to `[0, OXYGEN_POOL_START]` after each step. The Empty state and the `OnPlayerOxygenExpired` cascade are evaluated on the **final** `P` after all three steps, so a same-tick restore that lifts `P > 0` legitimately prevents the cascade (the "last-moment save" edge). This replaces the round-1 "arrival order" wording, which was network-arrival-dependent and therefore non-deterministic (RBI-5). No delta is dropped.
- **If `amount ≤ 0` or non-integer in a death spend:** reject as a no-op (defensive validation, even on the server-internal path).
- **If the band rises to BC4 (survival window opens):** RM does **nothing special to `P`** — the CR.9 entry clamp was removed (RBI-2). The squad simply meets the much steeper BC4 drain (8.0 u/s) from whatever pool the run left them; the steep drain is the anti-bunker fence (D.5).
- **If a 2-player squad takes a BC4 death (honest worst-case framing, IMPORTANT):** the death costs 72 oxygen, and with only one gatherer left the squad's `restoreRate_squad` halves (the bench cap no longer binds — 1 crafter ≈ 0.83 u/s) while BC4 drain stays flat at 8.0 (headcount-independent, OQ.3). Net ≈ −7.2 u/s after a death: a 2-player squad that takes even one BC4 death is, in practice, **near-unrecoverable** within the window. This is intended under the flat-drain ruling (OQ.3/OQ.4), **not** a bug — the deferred lever, if playtests show it is too brutal, is a squad-size-aware death cost **or** a last-stand oxygen floor, triggered when `aliveCount ≤ 2` (named here so it is not lost; owner game-designer/CD, OQ.3). **USER-RULED 2026-07-05 (cross-review D1): ACCEPT AS-DESIGNED with a BINDING playtest kill-criterion** — ship the flat-drain brutality; at the first vertical-slice playtest the qa-lead/game-designer MUST measure the 2-player post-BC4-death outcome, and **if the 2-player win rate after a BC4 death reads as effectively zero (no observed recoveries across the playtest's 2-player BC4-death samples), the last-stand oxygen floor (`aliveCount ≤ 2`) ships as the PRE-COMMITTED fallback** — no fresh ruling needed at that point; the decision is made now, gated on the data. Recorded at OQ.4.
- **Degenerate strategies (scanned, all non-exploitable):** *suicide-to-respawn* loses 72 units + 30 s of gather capacity for zero benefit; *Canister stockpiling* can't trivialize BC4 — **under the R3-3 per-squad cap** the squad's entire banked supply is one stack = 100 units `< 280` the window drains, so banked Canisters can fund at most 100 of the entry pool a coast needs (D.5(a); the Beacon hold, not oxygen, is the win condition); *hoard-then-dump* is bounded by the **per-squad** `ITEM_STACK_MAX` cap (R3-3) and the dead-player inventory lock; *coast-on-a-fat-pool* is **not** dismissed by assertion — the D.5 arithmetic shows it requires arriving at BC4 with `≥ 280` oxygen, of which banked items supply ≤ 100 and the remaining ≥ 180 must be a deep *carried* pool the D.6 countdown is constantly draining (net-negative from BC3 on for a full squad, every band for a 2-player squad), making a coast the *minority* outcome, not impossible (the honest framing of blocker 1/9; playtest-validated, OQ.9); *2-player flat-drain* is strictly **harder** (same drain, *and* `CRAFT_CONCURRENCY_CAP = 1` so net-negative even at BC1 — D.6), not easier.

**DP-2 is a coupling, not two independent safe ranges (blocker 5 — corrects the round-2 "no crash zones" overclaim).** `OXYGEN_POOL_START` (300–900) and `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` (60–90 at default pool) are individually plausible but **must not be chosen independently** — `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` is only valid *relative to the chosen pool*. Three of the four corners of the (pool 300–900) × (cost 60–90) box **violate** DP-2 `[0.10, 0.15]`: (300, 60) = 0.20 ✗, (300, 90) = 0.30 ✗, (900, 60) = 0.067 ✗, only (900, 90) = 0.10 ✓ sits on the boundary. So there is **no fixed numeric cost range that is safe across the whole pool range** — `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` must be re-derived as `[0.10, 0.15] × OXYGEN_POOL_START` whenever the pool changes. The **real defense** is the H.24 fail-fast init assertion (`DEATH_OXYGEN_COST / OXYGEN_POOL_START ∈ [0.10, 0.15]`, run does not start on violation), not the table's per-knob ranges. The old `OXYGEN_BC4_ENTRY_CAP` knob and its fragile 7.5-unit margin are gone with CR.9 (RBI-4); the remaining couplings above (D.5a, monotonic bands, `CANISTER < DEATH`) hold with healthy margins, but DP-2 specifically is a *relationship*, and the table's `DEATH_OXYGEN_COST` range is the default-pool instantiation, not an independent safe band.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

- **Audio (the shared breath):** a low, continuous respirator/breathing ambient layer keyed to pool state, intensifying as `P` falls; on **Critical** entry a distinct squad-wide alarm/heartbeat cue. Per the game's anti-pillar, all oxygen audio uses **fixed UI/non-positional channels** — it must never be a diegetic positional sound the predator's noise model could read. Canister restore = a brief relief-intake cue; a death deduction = a sharp "lurch" sting tied to the bar. Continuous drain has no per-tick sound.
- **Visual:** the single shared bar is the literal rendering of shared fragility (see UI). Critical = red pulse; Empty hands off to PC's death visual; restore = a bar tick-up flash; death = a visible lurch-down.
- **Accessibility floor (RBI-6 — the game-concept's own floor, BLOCKING for the HUD that consumes these triggers):**
  - **Critical state must not rely on red alone** — the red pulse requires a redundant **shape/icon** channel (e.g. a warning glyph + a text/numeric state label) so colorblind players read the crisis.
  - **The pulse must obey WCAG 2.3.1 — and RM's trigger-rate cap is NOT the visual-flash cap (blocker 5).** WCAG 2.3.1 bounds **visible flashes** to ≤ 3/s. RM only rate-limits **one trigger stream** — `OnOxygenStateChanged` — via `OXYGEN_STATE_CHANGE_MIN_INTERVAL = 0.334 s` (≤ 3 state-changes/s, blocker 6; see Tuning Knobs + H.50). RM does **NOT** rate-limit the other two flashing cues: `OnOxygenRestored` (Canister tick-up flash) and `OnOxygenDeducted` (death lurch) fire on real game events and can legitimately arrive **faster than 3/s** (e.g. several Canisters dumped in quick succession), and the HUD may also animate multiple frames per single trigger. **Therefore the WCAG 2.3.1 ≤ 3-flashes/s bound across the *combined* set of flashing cues (state pulse + restore flash + death lurch) is a HUD-side obligation, not satisfied by RM's trigger cap alone.** RM **names this obligation** (OQ.7): the HUD must coalesce/throttle the *aggregate* visual flash rate of all oxygen cues to ≤ 3/s — RM's per-stream `OXYGEN_STATE_CHANGE_MIN_INTERVAL` and same-tick `simultaneousCount` coalescing only *reduce* the trigger volume, they do not guarantee the visual bound. (RM rate-limiting the restore/death triggers themselves is rejected: dropping a real death/restore cue would mis-state the bar; the bound belongs to the rendering layer.)
  - **The principal pre-death warning must not be audio-only** — the alarm/heartbeat cue requires a **visual redundancy** (the Critical state's own visual treatment) so deaf/HoH players are not denied the warning.
  - **Coalescing rule:** when several death deductions or restores resolve in the same tick, RM emits the semantic event **once** with `simultaneousCount` = the *applied* count (for deaths, the `k` non-duplicate keys actually charged, not the raw queued N — blocker 6/H.44/CR.4); the HUD renders **one** coalesced cue, never N stacked flashes (prevents a 4-simultaneous-death strobe). This trims the *trigger* volume but does not by itself satisfy the WCAG visual bound (blocker 5 — see above).
- RM specifies the **state triggers and the distinct semantic events** (drain vs death-lurch vs canister-restore — RBI-5); the HUD and Audio GDDs own the actual asset specs, the WCAG-bounded animation, and the mix. *(Art/audio-director consult deferred — infrastructure; the bar's visual/sonic identity is a HUD + art-bible job.)*
